# The iPhone pitch

Practice opens on grass. The surface picker changes the ground in the current session. Tap the pitch to put the circle there (a tap on or beside a standing kubb aims at it), or drag anywhere to move the circle by the finger's travel; lifting snaps it to a kubb it is near. Press the dial near six, wind back on the left, flick through six and lift on the right. Bringing the arm back slowly lets it down. The solid band is the release angle; the striped band is the backswing (each with ink end-marks, so they read apart without their colours). Practice takes batons and the engine's `pointer` tune.

## Picture and clock

`KubbRenderer.PitchRenderer.draw(in:size:camera:scene:)` takes a top-left-origin Core Graphics context. Size is in points. The renderer saves and restores the caller's graphics state. Its only persistent geometry is the immutable, seeded ground texture and the six default stakes. A render never calls Lua.

- The camera is the engine's eye, orthonormal basis, focal length and principal point. In eye space, depth clips at 0.05 m. Projection divides right and up by depth, inverts screen y and applies the principal point. Landscape centres the 400 × 240 reference frame at `min(width/400, height/240)`. The portrait fit is described below. Sky and ground extend to every edge. The same transform casts drag points back onto z = 0.
- The scene accepts surface, set, posed items, advantage line, target, arc, thrower, raise footprints and punishment keep-out circles. The local z axis runs along each piece. Kubb, king, baton and stake extents, six stake positions, paint and team bands, light direction, hard shadows and near-plane clipping follow the web renderer. Nearby overlapping boxes use separating planes to correct depth order. The arc stops 0.45 m short of its target so the kubb stays visible.
- Every piece is drawn at the quaternion the engine gives, never stood up in Swift. From engine 1.0.0 a kubb standing plumb sits in its seat in the turf, leaning up to 2 degrees on grass, 3 on sand and 1.5 on snow, plumb on the Techno grid (kubb-engine docs/notes/physics.md "The seat"): the session's items and every recording already carry the lean, so the aim, a throw's first frame, the other side's throw, the minimap and the wide view need nothing of their own. The engine's own watched raises end on their entry's `seat` (docs/MATCH.md); a raise drawn from a need's plain poses (by hand, AUTO, another member's) ends plumb and takes its seat with the next throw's items.
- `PitchSession` owns the main-actor engine for practice and solo. A tap or target drag calls `setTarget` (`AimDrag`). A display tick consumes at most one pointer frame. `ThrowInput` isolates input sampling, lift and cancellation. The pointer implementation uses forward degrees from six, preserving touch boundaries and accumulating elapsed time between samples. Its stationary feed matches the web's stop detection. The display link sleeps on a still, unattended pitch and stops outside the active scene.
- The release frame already contains the applied throw. Decode its tracks, index moving bodies by id once, and retain static poses. Between samples, interpolate position and normalize the shortest-path quaternion blend. The session's one `showAt` call per moving frame supplies the camera, speed and dial. The typed playback response skips its redundant items array. The release tick can make both calls. Reaching rest holds the last picture for 0.8 s, then one `view` loads the next aim. The verdict stays `Pacing.verdictShown`, 3.5 s from the throw coming to rest, or goes at the first press of the aim (docs/MATCH.md).
- Timeline bursts follow world anchors. Flash, pop, the second KLAK, king crown and wood chips run on elapsed display time while the session can slow flight time. The engine supplies the flag and verdicts. Off-target words go first. The display face is Londrina Solid Black, with its OFL in the app bundle.

## Portrait framing

When height exceeds 1.05 times width, the scale is `1.3 × width / 400`. The far baseline sits at 41% of screen height (`PitchProjection.anchorAt`, the web's layout.js anchorAt; the owner, 2026-11-22: "I wonder if our camera view should be a touch lower so the kubbs we are aiming at are a little lower on the screen"): its frame y in each throw's aim camera (`PitchProjection.farFrame`, held to 40 to 180, 96 without one) is taken at the throw's adopt (`PitchScene.farFrameY`) and kept through its flight, so it is 41% from the baseline, from a Kid match's line and from a stance up to the advantage line alike. Before, frame y 112 sat at 33%, which put the far baseline at 31% from the baseline. The king now stands at about 64% and the near baseline below the screen; the clock, the tray and the near field kubbs keep their places and nothing aimed at goes under the clock (a close field kubb's ring is above its outer reach). The band under the header holds the minimap and the verdict (`WideLayout.verdictBand`), which stops 8 pt above the target (docs/MATCH.md). The projected target gets 40 reference pixels of horizontal room; the pan is bounded by the cropped reference frame. The current pan applies immediately and survives release. The same fit projects the wood, target circle, burst anchors and plan, and casts touch points back onto the pitch. Human placement uses the web's separate still framing at 42% height, with 1.8× magnification for raising and 1× for punishment. These are host presentation transforms on the same cameras. The raise camera (`RaiseCamera`, the web's scenes.js numbers) stands twice as far back as it first did (the owner, 2026-11-22: "When raising kubbs the camera view is too close. We need to see about twice as much to know how to raise them."): 2.3 m back across the kubb's length and 2.1 m up (1.15 and 1.05 before), looking at a point 0.24 m toward it (0.12), f 520, so the angle down is the same and twice the ground shows each way round the kubb (`RaiseFramingTests.theRaiseShowsTwiceAsMuch`: within 5% on every screen), its neighbours, the lines near it and often the king. The kubb is drawn half as big, about 44 pt long on the 16e upright; its touch target (the outline and the 24 pt press margin round it) stays at least 44 pt across every way and the ends' numbers keep their 17 pt circles, apart and on the screen, in a crowded raise of seven and beside the sideline too. From engine 1.4.0 a raise by hand has one held camera of the engine's instead (`need.camera`, kind "raise", at (0, -2.2, 3.4), f about 250, fitted to `SideView.box` in the letterbox fit, `PitchScene.heldRaise`): the whole half with the king, never moving from kubb to kubb, as on the web. A kubb is drawn about 3 to 20 pt long in it, so its press margin (24 pt) makes the target, the numbers are pushed at least 38.5 pt apart along it (`RaiseTouch.apart`) and a tap takes the nearest number; the other lying kubbs carry a dashed outline (`PracticeHUD.pickOutline`) and a tap on one picks it. A number that would cover another lying kubb moves, with its partner, square to the kubb (up the screen first) until both clear it by 25 pt (`RaiseTouch.clear`), so the next kubb stays in sight and their order along the kubb stays; a press on a number is that end's, never a pick. On a wide phone a drag needs only past half its 90 pt travel to stand the kubb, which `StillBarTests` hold above the bar. The watched raises (`RaiseShow`, `StoodShow`) and a 1.0 match keep `RaiseCamera`. The view from the player's side (`setView`, docs/MATCH.md) uses the same box for the other side's throws (kind "end"). The punishment scene is unchanged: it already shows the whole half a punishment kubb may go on. The centred far base kubb on fresh grass measures **15.84 pt** on the 390 pt iPhone 16e and **17.87 pt** on the 440 pt iPhone 17 Pro Max, toe to top before the ink outline (the scale did not change with the lower placement).

The dark stripe next to the former flight dial is the airborne baton's hard ground shadow. The renderer projects its shadow by (+0.55z, −0.30z) onto the ground. At tick 22 of the engine 0.5.0 practice throw, its centre in the original 390 × 844 framing is (317.44, 684.96) pt. A raster regression removes only the baton and measures the changed foreground pixels around that projection. The shadow stays, the dial and plan controls disappear during flight, and the pitch extends to the bottom.

## An iPad

Jamie: "Please take a moment to consider if now would be an okay time for us to build out an iPad version. We have our iPhone version, and I would definitely like to make one that is sized for iPad as well. I believe that our plate controls are well established and how the various landscape and portrait modes work. The idea of an iPad version would just be to give us a little bit bigger screen." So the app is universal and an iPad plays the same game on a bigger screen: no new controls, no other camera, no split views.

- **Orientations.** An iPad turns all four ways; an iPhone keeps portrait and landscape left and right. iPadOS 26 resizes every app's window (`UIRequiresFullScreen` is deprecated and ignored), so the app takes any window size and one scene (`UIApplicationSupportsMultipleScenes` false).
- **The same two rules.** A window taller than 1.05 times its width is laid out as the upright phone; any other is the web's wide arrangement above (`WideLayout`), both from the window's own size, never the device's. Upright the clock reaches its cap (radius 114) and the linear meter its 450 pt. On its side the clock is the web's S = min(0.28 W, 0.46 H, 380) (radius 137.7 pt on the iPad mini, 164.9 on the iPad Pro 13-inch) and the linear meter goes at its edge at 450 pt (track 378.3), standing on the bottom margin as before, so the rail and MENU keep their corners. The tray is at the far edge, mirrored for Meter side Left, in every window; ARC opens to its whole 300 pt on an iPad on its side; a window too short for the column under the header row brings its foot down toward the safe bottom, then shortens its plates (never under 48), as on the web. The raise and punishment bar is shown only in a wide window 460 pt high or less; taller windows keep the panel. Controls, plates and type keep their phone sizes; the pitch alone grows.
- **A window's controls.** In a window smaller than the screen iPadOS 26 puts close, minimise and full screen at the top leading corner and gives an app built for it no safe area for them. The app reads the safe area adapted to the window's corners (`UIView.edgeInsets(for: .safeArea(cornerAdaptation: .vertical))`) and adds its extra at the top to the root view controller's safe area (`WindowControls.swift`), so the rail and the cards sit clear of them (safe top 10 to 53 pt in a 700 × 486 window). Full screen and every iPhone add nothing.
- **Cards.** They keep the web's widths and stay centred: the score sheet min(640, 100%) (the web's `.sheet`), the tall raise and punishment panel 520 (`.panel`), Send feedback 560 (`.report`), the rest 330 as before. A phone keeps its sheet and feedback at full width, as before. A card taller than the window scrolls from its title (`FitOrScroll`), and the score sheet's notation starts at the top left of its box, not in its middle.
- **Turning or resizing mid-gesture** lets go as on a phone (`BoardView.layoutSubviews`, `PitchSession.cancelHeld`).

`WideLayoutTests` check both review iPads full screen, upright and on their side, both meters and both sides, and sweep window sizes (480 to 1400 pt wide and 380 to 1040 high, three sets of insets) for frames inside the safe area, nothing overlapping and the linear meter's room; `StillBarTests` and `ControlsTests` hold the bar and the raise touch on the iPads.

## Scenes

A scene (`PitchScene.look`) is one of the engine's catalogue (core/scenes, engine 0.14; `SceneID.catalogue`, held to the vendored engine's `scenes()` by `SceneTests.theCatalogueIsTheEnginesOwn`): Park (`day`), Beach, Camping (`night`), Techno (`neon`), Minnesota, Tournament and Black & White (`bw`). The engine decides a match's surface and set through it (Beach sand, Minnesota snow and its set, the Tournament the USA set, Techno the grid, Black & White any of grass, sand and snow); the app draws what the view says. The day's path in `PitchRenderer` is untouched: its five snapshots do not move. `SceneRenderer.swift` draws Camping and Techno in the order of the web's `drawWorld` (sky, ground, lines, pieces, effects) with the web's numbers and colours, `SceneWorlds.swift` the daylight scenes and Black & White, and `SceneID.shown` turns any id it does not know into the day (`SceneID.drawn` lists the scenes drawn).

The scenery stands off the pitch (outside |x| 3.5 m, or past y 10.5 m) and is drawn before the pieces, so it never hides a piece or where a throw lands. Like the rough's texture and the light, it is fixed in the frame drawn, the thrower's: the sea is always ahead of whoever throws, as Camping's moon always is.

- **Beach**: a raked sand pitch on pale dry sand, its lines a navy rope (5.2:1 on the sand; white on sand is 1.4:1), the sea from 14.5 m (6.5 m past the far baseline) with a wet strip, a scalloped foam edge that rolls a little and pale swell lines, a summer sky with flat clouds, umbrellas, towels and a ball off the sides. Sand puffs where a piece comes down hard.
- **Minnesota**: packed snow in stripes on drifted snow, blue lines (4.9:1), snowy pines and bare birches round the clearing and drifts at its edge, an overcast winter sky, snow falling over the picture (none under Reduce Motion). The Minnesota set. Snow sprays where a piece comes down hard.
- **Tournament**: a deeper mown grass (the white lines 3.1:1 on its stripes) in four-metre mowing squares, bunting on wooden poles between the pitches and across the far end (its posts at x ±5 and ±11, never behind a target), a scoreboard of the kubbs each side has standing, tents, and neighbouring pitches drawn by one function from each neighbour's data (`NeighbourPitch`: where it is, its pieces in its own frame, its board; no people: the owner, 2026-11-22, "Tournament mode should not have people. Just pitches with live play."; `Painter.neighbours`): live from engine 0.15.1 ("The Tournament's neighbours" below), and the four fixed pitches at x ±8 and ±16 under Reduce Motion or until the live field answers. The USA set. No championship's name or logo.
- **Black & White**: the Playdate's look (playdate-kubb's render/world.lua) on any of its surfaces: white sky and pitch, the rough's 8 × 8 pattern by surface (grass dashes, sand stipple, snow sprinkle) under the far edge but the pitch's footprint, its phase on the far baseline's middle, lines two Playdate pixels wide (`projection.k` is a Playdate pixel), and the day's pieces made 1-bit by the painter (`Painter.mono`: every paint pure black or white by lightness, a faint one left out), with no team bands and no antialiasing. `SceneLooksTests.blackAndWhiteIsOneBit` checks every pixel on all three surfaces. Its skin (`SceneSkin.mono`) is black and white: go inverted, Team A white with black type, Team B black with white type; the dimming behind a card is a checker (`SkinDim`), never grey.
- **Techno's grid** (engine 0.14's `grid` surface, the web's NEON grid): near-black and glossy under the horizon with a sheen 22% of the height deep where it meets the haze, a faint grid a metre apart (0.55 of the other surfaces' grid), the pitch a pane a shade lighter, cyan lines, and a mirror of what stands on it (each piece turned over the ground, drawn at 0.26, no shadow, no spin).

- **Night**: a sky graded from 0x02040b to 0x16264a, a milky band, 5,200 steady and twinkling stars, a full moon with its halo, a moonlit clearing on the pitch in seven tones, the lines' glow and pieces lit from the moon with a glow round each. A throw leaves stardust behind the thrown piece (`SceneEffects`, the web's Stage.fx).
- **Neon**: black, a grid of light on the ground, a horizon band, pieces edged in light with coloured bands, a ribbon behind the thrown piece and sparks on a hit.
- **The skin**: `SceneSkin` holds the web's NIGHT.skin and NEON.skin for the plates, cards, sliders, STAND, both meters, the bursts and the HUD's plaques; the app reads it through `SkinStore`. `SceneSkinTests` checks every pair of words and ground at 7:1 or better. The day is the 1.0 system's tokens (AGENTS.md "The 1.0 design"); its meters, bursts and STAND keep the app's colours from before scenes.
- **Ground impacts** (`Impacts.swift`, the web's impacts.js): the session asks for them once per Lua state (`setImpacts`), and each recording's `timeline.impacts` lists where pieces met the ground hard. Techno rings: a circle on the ground growing from 0.06 m to 0.22 + 0.17 × speed m (at most 0.95) over 0.75 s of the throw's clock (slow motion included), in the piece's side's light (the king his own, the baton white), with a bloom of 7 and 3.5 pt, none nearer the camera than 2.2 m; each ring of a tenth of the strength or more taps lightly (`PitchHaptic.impact`), but where the baton's own landing already does. Beach and Minnesota puff a small cartoon cloud (three discs of sand or snow with the ink line, swelling, rising 0.12 m and shrinking away over 0.55 s, never over 11 pt across) where a piece comes down faster than 1.4 m/s.
- **Reduce Motion**: no stardust trail and no ribbon, fewer and shorter puffs, no hit flash, rings that hold 45% of their size and fade where they struck, puffs that only shrink, no falling snow.
- **The Kid line** (a Kid match, engine 0.15's `aim.kidY`): short dots across the pitch (0.05 m on, 0.09 off, the web's), never drawn over the advantage line itself, with a traffic cone 0.3 m tall just off each sideline (x ±2.85: orange with a white band, Black & White's white with a black band, Techno's lit), in every scene and on the minimap (dots). The thrower stands on it, so the aim's own view is past it; the side view, the wide view, the minimap and STAND show it.
- **A spin the frames cannot show** (`Spin.swift`, the web's spinAmount and drawSpin; a close drive, engine 0.16, does not turn and is never drawn as one): each shown frame measures each piece's turn since the frame before, split into the twist about its long axis and the swing. Past 30 degrees a frame of twist (fully at 50; a square section repeats every quarter turn, so an eighth a frame reads backwards; a baton's twist never shows) or 60 of swing (fully at 100: end over end a half turn repeats), the piece is drawn with four in-between poses from where it was, each at 0.42 at full blur, its own pose at 0.45, back to the plain drawing as it slows. SPIN at its most launches a kubb at about 69 rad/s, 66 degrees a frame at 60 Hz: a full spin; at 120 Hz the turn a frame halves to 33 degrees, a touch of blur; slow motion shows none. Black & White draws the poses solid. The physics is never touched (`SpinTests`).
- **Glows** are the web's glow.js: one round sprite per colour, drawn once and stamped (`Glow.swift`). The geometry, colours and effect counts follow the web's code; the stars' exact pixels and the antialiasing are Core Graphics' own.

**Making it fast enough.** Core Graphics on iOS rasterizes on the CPU, after `draw(_:)` returns (`CABackingStoreUpdate` on the main thread), so the app's FrameTiming "update+draw" does not include it; the display callback's spacing does. Two things were slow and are now not: CG shades a linear gradient pixel by pixel, so the night sky and the neon's horizon band are filled as runs of whole device rows of one 8-bit colour (`rowGradient`); and thousands of tiny antialiased squares, so the steady stars are whole device pixels of about their own area, filled without antialiasing in one call. The picture is the same to the eye.

## The Tournament's neighbours

From engine 0.15.1 the pitches round the player's play computer-against-computer matches of their own, as scenery (kubb-engine docs/SESSION.md "Neighbours", docs/CLIENTS.md "0.15.1 on top", docs/notes/tournament.md). `TournamentNeighbours` (Neighbours.swift) runs them on an engine of their own, `Engine.neighbours()`: a second Lua state from the same bundle with the host's clock `__kubbClock` (monotonic milliseconds, `CLOCK_UPTIME_RAW`, a C function of the Swift target, so CLua and CMusl and their flags are untouched) set before the boot chunk, and no `useTune` or `setClient`. It never shares a state with the player's engine, and every call on it is made on a serial queue of its own, never the player's engine's and never waited on by the main thread.

- **When.** `GameModel.syncNeighbours` (on every screen change, the app's phase, a Reduce Motion change, and each frame) asks `TournamentNeighbours.want`: only a match, practice or watched match in the Tournament scene, on the screen or paused under its menu, sheet, how to throw, a throw report or settings from inside it. It opens with the match's seed (its snapshot's `opts.seed`, `PitchSession.neighbourSeed`; a practice has a seed of its session's), `scene: "tournament"` and the grid in every layout, upright, on its side and in the watcher's wide view (the owner: "a grid of pitches that are all live"; the engine's `row` for a phone held sideways leaves every neighbour outside this thrower's camera, so the app never asks for it). Leaving (home, the options card, a sealed match), a new seed, or the app in the background closes it; coming back reopens it with the same seed, so the same tournament starts again. Another scene never opens it, and under Reduce Motion the second engine is never made.
- **Each frame.** The board's display link moves the match clock on (it stops while the board does: a menu over the match pauses the neighbours) and asks for `advance({ now, budgetMs: 2 })` unless the one before is still running; answers are taken in at the next frame. A failed call leaves the neighbours standing as they were and the match untouched. The match's own hash is unchanged with the neighbours running (`NeighbourTests.thePlayersMatchIsUntouched`).
- **The model.** `NeighbourField` keeps each pitch's items, replaced by a beat that carries them, and plays a `throw`'s recording through the session's own `Playback`: `NeighbourRecording.recording(items:)` makes a `Recording` of the pitch's current items (the thrown piece, not among them, added at its first pose), no timeline (so no sound or haptics), and at its end the last poses are the new items, which equal the engine's `view` to the millimetre (`NeighbourTests.theFieldOpensPlaysAThrowAndCloses`).
- **Drawing.** Each pitch at its `offset`, the whole field turned half round about (0, 4) while the board draws in Team B's frame (`PitchScene.frameSide`, from one function, `PitchSession.viewFrame(of:)`, at every adopt: the need's side for an aim, a throw, a raise or a punishment kubb (the defender's, whose poses the engine gives in its frame), and for a finished match, live, resumed, reopened or adopted from a pitch, the frame the engine holds the match in, its snapshot's `side`; a practice is Team A's; a throw's recording plays in its thrower's until the next adopt; the watcher's wide view is the world frame and unturned, but for a raise or punishment scene, which keeps its close camera on the need's poses in the defender's frame for a watcher too: the engine's wide view re-frames items and playback, never a need; `NeighbourFrameTests`), its pieces at a far pitch's detail (no shadows), the USA set, the six stakes and its scoreboard (the kubbs each side has standing). No people (2026-11-22; before, up to three a team walked, threw and cheered) and no names. A pitch wholly off the screen is left out, and the board draws again for the neighbours only when something on the screen changes (`needsDraw`); while only the neighbours move it asks for 30 frames a second (`BoardView.calmRate`).
- **Kept calm** (`Calm`, Neighbours.swift; the web's neighbours.js numbers). With no one to hold it, a thrown piece is drawn only once it is 0.3 m from where it left (`Calm.airborne`; the web draws it from its release pose), so it never hangs at an empty hand; the kubbs a `raise` beat stands up rise from where they lay over 0.6 s each, eased, 0.15 s apart, and on a `setup` beat (a fresh pitch set out) every kubb and the king rise from lying where they stand over 0.5 s, 0.07 s apart; batons gathered on a walk just go. Nothing else moves between throws. With the people gone nothing stands taller than a kubb past the player's targets: the scoreboards stand behind each neighbour's far end and outside the player's pitch, the bunting's posts beside it, and with the live grid the tents stand back beyond its far row. Upright, with the far baseline at 41% (above), the pitch ahead and the bunting across the far end show above the targets, beyond them.
- **Heard** (`NeighbourSounds`): a knock where a kubb goes over, louder for the king, a softer, lower one where a thrown piece lands, quiet and far, panned, three at once at most, never round the player's own knock; docs/FEEL.md "The other pitches".
- **What the thrower sees.** The thrower's camera frames the player's own pitch, so in the aim the field shows at the edges: upright and on its side, the pitch straight ahead playing on behind the bunting (its baseline along the top of a phone on its side; the corner pitches and the tents stay outside the aim's frame there, so nothing beside looms over the targets); during a throw the flight camera brings in more, and the watcher's wide view shows the most (the grid there too: the pitches ahead and behind at the ends of the frame, those beside along its far side).
- **Cost** (simulator, iOS 26.2, which renders at 60 Hz; release build, 600-frame windows): the field adds 0.8 to 1.0 ms a frame to the drawing upright (16e 0.82 to 0.90 mean, p95 1.01; 17 Pro Max 0.97 to 1.04, p95 1.21; a debug build 1.2 to 1.8), 0.7 to 0.9 on its side with the grid (16e 0.70 to 0.74, p95 0.96; 17 Pro Max 0.79 to 0.86, p95 1.04), and 1.75 to 1.9 on the iPad Pro 13-inch upright (p95 2.35 to 2.7, the largest canvas, where the simulator's own frame is about 6 ms); `advance` takes 0.09 to 0.11 ms on average on its queue (p99 1.9, the budget), with no frame left out. At 120 Hz that is about 0.1 to 0.12 s of drawing a second while the field moves on the screen, at the calm 30 Hz about 0.03. `MATCH_REVIEW=neighbourstiming` prints these (`NeighbourTiming`, FrameTiming.swift).
- Review pictures (and for the watched raise and the verdict: `--only raisetip` with `SIMCTL_CHILD_RAISE_AT=<s>`, an automatic raise frozen that far in, and `--only verdictaim`, the verdict still up over the next aim): `--scene tournament --only neighbours,neighbourswide,neighboursflight` (a neighbour's throw half way and in view in the thrower's view, the watcher's wide view, and the player's own flight); `NeighbourLooksTests` keeps pictures under `SCENE_LOOKS_OUT` and prints the drawing's cost.

## Home

The owner (2026-11-27): "Instead of a static image, I'd like to have the Kubb Engine playing a game in the background. Pick the standard 'Park' scene so it looks 'normal'. If we can have the game minimize idle time it would be good." Behind home a real match plays: the computer against the computer (`cpu: "both"`, the web's watch mode's `newMatch`), Pro on both sides, raising automatic, no wind, the Park scene, a random seed, the first side alternating, at most 40 turns. When it ends the next starts with no card.

- **Its own engine, off the main thread** (`HomeMatch`, HomeMatch.swift), as the neighbours run: a Lua state of its own (`Engine(appVersion:)`, the newest carried engine) on a serial utility queue of its own, never the player's engine and never waited on by a frame. The queue plays each throw headless: `frame({ dt: 0.1, speed: 8 })` until the release (the computer's 0.9 s think and 0.6 s swing are never shown), then the flight's camera from `showAt` every 1/30 s of real time at the throw's own pace (its slow motion round a hit kept), then `need`. The main thread gets a finished throw (the pitch as aimed, the aim camera, the recording, the camera samples) and only plays it back through `Playback`, mixing the two nearest camera samples, and draws it with `PitchRenderer.draw` in the day. Two throws are made ahead at most; a debug build makes one in about 70 ms on a Mac.
- **The camera** is the thrower's, as a solo match shows it: the aim camera, then the flight camera following the baton in and round the hit, the far baseline at 41% of a tall screen (`farFrameY`) and the aim's focus (`focusX`), so it reads as the game on every screen. The watcher's wide view was the other choice; its pieces are a few points tall on a phone, too small to sell anything behind the buttons.
- **Little idle time** (`HomeTune`): after a throw rests 0.5 s (a match's 0.8) the next throw's aim shows at once, the kubbs the engine raised rising into place from where the last throw left them (turned round when the other end throws now; `Calm.rises`, the neighbours' 0.6 s each, 0.15 s apart), then holds 0.35 s (the same thrower) or 0.8 s (the other end, the camera turned round) and throws. A finished match rests 2.2 s; the next one's kubbs stand up (`Calm`'s setup) and its first throw holds 0.4 s. In a half minute of play about 11 throws are shown and 3 frames in 4 move.
- **Scenery only.** No sound, no haptics, no verdict, burst, card or HUD, no names; nothing kept (it never touches the kept solo match, the options, the name, `pitch.throwsMade` or the coach's counts) and nothing sent (`HomeMatchTests.itKeepsNothing`).
- **When.** `GameModel.syncHome` on every screen change, the app's phase and a Reduce Motion change: on while home shows; paused while the app is in the background on home (the match and its Lua state kept, its clock still, nothing made); off everywhere else, which lets the Lua state go, so a second one exists only while home shows. Under Reduce Motion it is never started and home shows the still pitch under it, as before.
- **Drawing.** `HomeBackdropView` (Home.swift) is its own view and display link over the pitch board (which stays still on home), at most 60 frames a second, drawing again only when the picture changed; nothing until the first throw has come (about 0.2 s), the still pitch showing through. Simulator, `HOME_TIMING=1` logs its main-thread cost every 600 frames.
- **The controls** never stand on the grass: the masthead (KUBB over THROWING WOOD) is the match header's plate across the top with SETTINGS inside it at the right, the header's own button as MENU is (a gear and the word; the gear alone where the word does not fit), and the three ways to play are one card at the foot (the card face: paper, 3 pt line, radius 8, 5 pt shadow): upright PLAY ONLINE and PRACTICE side by side over PLAY THE COMPUTER, the screen's one go button, last; on a wide screen the three in one row, go at the right. The card's foot line is the version (`VersionLabel.text`). At the accessibility sizes the card stacks and the screen scrolls. VoiceOver: the title (a header), PLAY THE COMPUTER, PLAY ONLINE, PRACTICE, Settings, the version; the pitch is hidden from it.
- **Review pictures** (`--only home`): `MATCH_REVIEW=home` plays the match of seed 7 headless to its third throw, 15% through its flight (`HomeReview`; `SIMCTL_CHILD_HOME_THROW` and `SIMCTL_CHILD_HOME_AT` choose another moment), draws it once and holds it, then signals the tool, so the picture is the same every time (`HomeMatchTests.aSeedIsTheSameMatch`).

## The app icon

The owner's direction (2026-11-22): "I want the icon to be a zoomed in profile of a Kubb King and where you just see the top 1/3rd of the king with the crown framed in the icon. Then we make alternative icons with every scene and its king image as well." Built, awaiting his look.

- **Drawn by the game.** `AppIconArt` (KubbRenderer) stands the renderer's own king (`Wood.swift`, the same faces, crown cuts, band, line and ink) upright on the pitch's middle and draws it with `PitchRenderer.draw` in the icon's scene, so its ground, sky, light and the king's paint are that scene's. Nothing is drawn by hand for the icon.
- **The crop.** The camera stands 0.53 m off, level with the crown's notches (0.26 m up, 0.4 cm above), turned 30 degrees so the king's shaded side shows beside his face: the profile. A long lens (f 1200) keeps it flat. The frame holds the crown, his band and the line under it, about the king's top 0.13 m of 0.30, with the crown's tips about a quarter of the way down against the sky and the horizon behind the notches. His diamond is just out of frame.
- **The line.** The icon is laid out at 128 pt and scaled to its pixels, so the renderer's 3.4 pt ink outline is 27 px of a 1024 icon and still a line at 40 and 60 px on the home screen.
- **Park** (`day`) is the primary, `AppIcon`, in the single-size 1024 format with its `appearances`: **dark** is the same picture taken to dusk (a navy wash at 80% over the world) with the king drawn again over it as bright as by day, and **tinted** is the dark look in greys (a grey PNG, the king the brightest thing, on a dark ground) for iOS to tint. The other six scenes are alternates, each king on its scene's own ground in its scene's kubb set (the catalogue's): **Beach** (`AppIcon-Beach`), **Camping** (`AppIcon-Camping`, the glowing king, the stars), **Techno** (`AppIcon-Techno`, the king edged in light over the grid), **Minnesota** (`AppIcon-Minnesota`, the navy head and north star in falling snow), **Tournament** (`AppIcon-Tournament`, the USA colours under the bunting) and **Black & White** (`AppIcon-BlackWhite`, one bit). Every PNG is opaque, with no corners cut: iOS masks it.
- **A scene's icon is one line** in `AppIconArt.icons` (its scene id and icon set name) once the scene is drawn and named (`SceneID.drawn`, `SceneID.shownNames`), then `scripts/make-app-icons.sh`. Only scenes this build draws have one; `AppIconTests` checks the table against the scenes and against the catalogue, and that the committed PNGs are what the generator draws today.
- **Making them.** `scripts/make-app-icons.sh` (Xcode only) runs the package's `make-app-icons` tool, which rewrites each table entry's icon set in `ThrowingWood/Assets.xcassets`. Commit the PNGs it writes.
- **In the build.** The target sets `ASSETCATALOG_COMPILER_INCLUDE_ALL_APPICON_ASSETS`, so every icon set in the catalogue goes in as an alternate (`CFBundleAlternateIcons`) with no list to keep. Settings' App icon row (`AppIconSettings`) asks for one with `setAlternateIconName` (nil for Park). iOS answers "try again" while its notice of the last change is still up, and sometimes changes the icon all the same, so the row asks again a few times and trusts the icon iOS then reports; only if iOS kept another does the tile go back, with "This device kept the icon it had."
- **Review pictures.** On the simulator `REVIEW_APP_ICON=<scene id>` chooses that icon once Settings is up, `REVIEW_CARD_FOOT` opens a card's scrolling rows at their foot, and the solo tool's `matchsettings` shot opens Settings from a match's menu in its scene (`--scene`). A simulator takes one icon change per boot when nobody dismisses iOS's notice.

## The watcher's wide view

A board that watches a match (`PitchSession.watching`: an observer, or a member with
no side) asks the engine for its wide view, `setWide {box}`, in the box the web's
layout.js wideBox gives (`WatchView.box`: the 400 x 240 frame fitted whole, less the
HUD's top, 132 pt upright or 70 on its side, and a 16 pt margin, inside the safe
area), and draws everything in the world frame through the plain letterbox
(`PitchProjection(letterbox:)`). Where the smallest base kubb would be under 2 pt
(`observeMinPx`) it keeps the thrower's view. No minimap and no meter in it. Players
keep the thrower's view; a raise or punishment scene keeps its own close look.

The free camera (`FreeCamera.swift`, the web's freecam.js) starts at the engine's
wide camera, untouched, and orbits a point on the pitch: yaw, pitch 4 to 86 degrees,
2.5 to 24 m out, the eye at least 0.35 m up, the point within x -4..4 and y -2.5..10.5.
The lens (f, cx, cy) never changes. `FreeCam.lookAt` is core/camera.lua's lookAt,
the same arithmetic (`FreeCameraTests` pins it against the engine's Lua bit for bit).
One finger turns (0.006 rad a point) and raises or lowers (0.004); two fingers
pinch, twist and slide the ground. RESET VIEW, low on the left once the view has
moved, eases back over 0.45 s (at once with Reduce Motion). "Drag to look around.
Pinch to zoom." shows until the first move or 8 s. Nothing is stored. Scenes draw
through it.

### Drawing details

- Quartz rasterizes the ink edges and dashes. Grass blades use short batches of filled, hard-edged shapes and sand/snow specks have square ends. Their positions still use the web seed. The playfield and dial use at most two backing pixels per point for the moving-frame budget. SwiftUI controls and text use display resolution.
- Batons have 24-sided circular cross-sections with a projected cap and highlight, plain wood in every set with no bands (Jamie, 2026-11-20: "Batons are always plain and never changed for the set."). The web constructs analytic ellipses. End-grain rings and pith, crown interiors, cheese spots and some bevels use simpler paths. Small differences appear on close wood and around silhouettes. These are presentation approximations. Positions in moving pieces use the recording's 1e−5 quantization; the session camera uses its full-precision playback.
- The provisional dial uses the web's angle convention, zones, prompt, power and release targets and last-throw marks. It has a simpler face, hand, ticks and knob. The native burst uses its own text metrics, short chip animation and crown path. Labels use the system face, display words use Londrina. The native screen stacks its verdict under the heading. These UI differences are intentional.

## The drive

Engine 0.16 extends a baton's ARC down through flat to a downward drive (kubb-engine
docs/notes/throw.md "The drive"; the web's plan-sliders.js and tray.js are the reference).

- **The range** is `aim.controls.arc` whenever ARC is drawn: `min` the throw's floor (about 0
  at 1 m, 0.3 at 1.5 m, 5 at 2 m, 12 at 4 m, 15 from about 5 m), `knee` 15. It moves with the
  circle and the stance, so `PitchSession.replan` draws again when the control changes, the
  plate open or not (`DriveTests.theRangeFollowsTheCircleWithThePlateOpen`).
- **The track** with a drive part (`PlanControl.driveFraction` > 0) is two straight scales
  joined at the knee: the floor to 15 on the lowest 0.3 × (15 − floor) / 15 of the run, 15 to
  165 on the rest. `ArcSlider` hatches the part in the scene's wood at 55% (4 pt stripes every
  8, at 135 degrees, the web's), from 3 pt under the run's foot to the knee, under a 3 pt ink
  line across the plate at the knee, and names it DRIVE up the lane of marks, reading upward,
  where the part is at least 54 pt tall (`ArcDrive.wordFrom`; Londrina 14: the web's 10 px word
  shows from 44 px, and nothing in the app is under 14 pt). The marks are the knee's and above,
  on the upper scale; none under it. At 8 m the floor is the knee and the slider is 15 to 165
  as before, no hatching.
- **The words**: the band, `DRIVE −5°` under the knee (a true minus; `ARC 22°` over it), on the
  closed plate and the open one; VoiceOver "Arc, 5 degrees down, drive" on the closed plate
  (label Arc, value "5 degrees down, drive") and "Arc 5 degrees down, drive" on the open
  slider. The closed plate's picture for a drive is the web's: from the hand high at the left,
  dashed, down into a kubb's face at the right, its middle lower as the loft goes under 0.
  The held caption: "A DRIVE GOES IN LOW AND HARD, WITH NO FLIP." (a baton over the knee "A
  HIGHER ARC DROPS IN STEEPER. A LOWER ARC DRIVES IN HARDER.", a kubb "A LOWER ONE SKIDS IN.").
- **The side view** draws the engine's `aim.arc` point by point, as for any throw, so a flight
  leaving the hand going down falls all the way to the kubb's face (cut 0.45 m short of the
  target along the ground only, as the web's arcInAir); nothing assumes it rises first.
- **No flip**: under the knee the engine eases the baton's turn to none, so a close drive's
  poses turn under the spin drawing's 60 degrees a frame and `Spin.swift` leaves it alone
  (`DriveTests.aCloseDriveDoesNotFlip`); nothing in the app draws a turn of its own.
- **Pictures** (`python3 tools/solo-snapshots.py <app> --drive`, with `--phone`, `--udid`,
  `--landscape` or `--ipad`): ARC closed and open at 1.5 m (its default and its floor), 4 m and
  8 m, the caption on the drive part, a drive in flight, its verdict, a SHORT one and the
  coach after it, the meter at a drive on both meters and sides, the inkast's meter (a thrown
  kubb's narrower bars), and frames of the computer's raise watched (`cpuraise`, `STOOD_AT` how far through the show, 0 to 1).
  They stand on the web's advantage position (`Fixtures/solo/advantage.json`, `DRIVE_AT`,
  `DRIVE_FLOOR`).

## The meter

The throw's meter is the player's choice, kept on the device: MENU (practice) or the match menus, Meter, Clock or Linear (`pitch.meter`, clock by default), and Side, Left or Right (`pitch.meterSide`, right by default) for the linear meter. `KubbRenderer/ThrowMeter.swift` holds the geometry and which touches each meter claims, and both feed the same `PointerDial`: the clock its finger's angle about the centre, the linear meter `meterPoint(arm:)` for the knob's height. The engine and its `pointer` tune do not know which meter is on the screen.

- **The clock** keeps its place and size: radius `min(0.22 W, 0.12 H, 114)` pt, centre 42 pt above the bottom safe area. Its colours are the app's own (system red and grey), not the web's palette.
- **The linear meter** (`LinearMeterHUD.swift`) is a port of the web's `src/render/linear.js`, its tune number for number: two columns in one ink frame with a hard shadow, the lane (targets) toward the screen's centre and the channel (the wood knob) toward the edge, the prompt a tab at the knob's home. The knob's home is the clock's 6, the middle of the track; the backswing runs down, toward the player, and the forward swing up, on one scale. The box is 0.2 of the width (64 to 96 pt) and 0.47 of the height, at least 390 pt and at most 450 pt, the web's, and stands 7 pt off the edge and the bottom safe area, under the minimap. An upright screen that cannot give it 320 pt of track (a short phone) shows the clock. On a wide screen the box has the whole safe height and needs 260 pt of track ("A phone on its side" above). Its words: DRAW BACK, FLING!, BACK TO MIDDLE, with the throw's distance on an inverted tab beside DRAW BACK (the tab's room is 6 tab heights, `LinearMeter.tabRoom`, and the nudge keeps clear of it). The tray stands at the other edge (`Tray.uprightEdge`), so nothing of it is under the meter or its tabs. The plates are SwiftUI views over the board, so a press on one never reaches `BoardView` (never a throw), and a press on the meter never reaches a plate.
- **The bars go by distance** (engine 0.13): wide for a short throw, narrow for a long one (5.6 and 21 degrees either side at 4 m, 2 and 7.5 at 8 m), the same for every person. `dial.powerLo..powerHi` and `releaseLo..releaseHi` are the bars for the plan as it stands, and change whenever the circle or the stance moves; the session's aim calls read the new dial with the move (`PitchSession.replan`), and every meter (the clock, the linear meter, the nudge's swing, the coach's stage) draws from `pitch.dial` on each paint and keeps no copy, so a moved plan is drawn with its own bars (`ControlsTests.theBarsAndTheArcsDefaultFollowThePlan`). A release past its bar comes back HIGH or LOW and PULLED LEFT or RIGHT; the verdict shows the misses first (`MeterTests.aReleasePastItsBarIsPulledAndSaysSo`).
- **Red** starts at `max(overDeg, powerHi)` on both meters: the engine's overpower cost counts from the power target's top (core/throwplan), so a long throw's target past 11 o'clock is never drawn as overpowered.
- **A drive** (engine 0.16, "The drive" below) is planned hard: at ARC's floor its power band is far round, about 134 to 146 at 1.5 m and 4 m (125 to 147 across the close throws), under the overpowered mark at 150, so the red starts at the engine's mark and never over the band, on both meters, both sides, upright and on its side (`DriveTests.theMeterAtADriveKeepsItsBandClearOfTheOverpoweredZone`).
- **The last throw** (the engine's `lastBack` and `lastStop`) is a faded ghost of the knob where it was, 60% of its size at 45% opacity, so it reads as "where your last throw went" and never as a target. The web draws the same.

### Touches

In `BoardView.touchesBegan`, in this order. Anything a step does not claim falls to the next.

1. Raise and punishment phases: the raise is a touch on the kubb (a tap, a tap by an end's number, or a drag that tips it; docs/MATCH.md), punishment as before.
2. `MeterLayout.first`: the clock's ring, within 1.25 radii of its centre (the throw at once, as always); the linear knob, 46 pt up or down from home, across the body and 6 pt either side of it (the throw at once, grabbed relatively: the press point is home); the rest of the linear body and its shadow (swallowed, never a throw). On a wide screen the knob grabs across its channel only (6 pt past the body's outer edge), and the rest of the body is throw zone (`.zone`: a tap aims at what is behind it, a drag strokes), since there it stands over the far baseline; `MeterLayout.press` says what a claim comes to once the press lifts or moves.
3. The thrower's stance mark (44 pt), dragged directly. STAND is a SwiftUI plate in the tray and never reaches `BoardView`. With a tray plate open, any press here closes it and does nothing else (a tap outside closes an open plate).
4. `MeterLayout.after`: beside the clock's ring, 1.25 to 2.1 radii; the linear meter's throw zone, y ≥ 0.6 of the height upright (`zoneFrom`), and on a wide screen the meter's half, top to bottom: x ≥ 0.5 W with the meter at the right, x ≤ 0.5 W at the left (`wideZone`). The lower 40% of a phone on its side is about 156 pt, too shallow for a whole draw back and flick; the meter's half gives the thumb the screen's height, and the tray is on the other half, where a drag still moves the aim. Beside the ring where a tap could not aim (`tapCouldAim`, the tap rule `target(tap: true)` uses), the press is the throw at once. Otherwise the press waits: one that moves more than 10 pt (`MeterLayout.tapSlop`) is the throw, a stroke from the knob's home for the zone, and one that does not is a tap on the pitch.
5. The aim (`AimDrag`): a tap puts the circle there, a drag anywhere else moves it from where it is. A tap that waited in step 4 aims the same way (`tapAim`).

A stroke from the zone or beside the ring feeds the engine the press on one frame and the finger on the next, so a quick first move still arms the swing. A finger beside the ring is drawn as a spoke from the knob to it.

### The nudge

When a player is stuck on their own throw, a ghost of the knob makes one good throw on the meter with a ghost fingertip where the finger goes: a slow draw back (0.9 s), a beat, a fast flick (0.25 s), then a second's rest, until the next press. `ThrowNudge.swift` (pure, the web's `src/game/nudge.js` numbers) says when and where on the timeline; `NudgeHUD.swift` draws it. It counts only eligible time (the player's own aim in practice or a match, no finger down, the side view closed and no slider held, the dial idle; a held slider counts as input) on the wall clock, and the display link sleeps until it is due. A device with fewer than 3 throws (`pitch.throwsMade`, capped at 9) is nudged after 8 s in the aim without a press on the meter; any device after two presses on the meter in a row that made no throw (0.8 s after the last); a learned one after 30 s with no input. A tap in the throw zone or beside the ring keeps it away 1.5 s. The fingertip is beside the ring (1.6 radii) on the clock in practice; in a match the plan's sliders sit beside the ring, so it presses the knob instead. On the linear meter it is in the throw zone on the meter's inner side, between the two slider columns; on a wide screen it is in the meter's half, halfway from the centre line to the prompt tab, level with the knob's home. While the engine says WIND BACK the plate follows the ghost. Reduce Motion shows a still picture at the back with the strokes' arrows. Drawing only: the ghost is never fed to the engine.

### The coach line

While a device is learning (fewer than 3 throws, `pitch.throwsMade`) a few words sit just over the meter's prompt and follow the stroke: PULL BACK TO THE STRIPES at rest, NO RUSH GOING BACK while drawing back short of the power band, NOW FLICK FAST AND STOP ON THE SOLID MARK from the band through the flick (the marks named by shape, the owner's decision in the 1.0 review). After a throw of this player's own that the engine called SLOW, the next throw is coached on any device, led by SLOW FLICK. FLICK FASTER. `KubbRenderer/Coach.swift` is the web's `src/game/coach.js` (its tests ported in `CoachTests`); `CoachHUD.swift` draws it in the 1.0 design's one style: an inverted ink chip, paper capitals in Inter bold 15, the slow lead on its own first line in the same chip (never three pills at once), over the clock's prompt, centred, or from the linear meter's inner edge over its tab. On a wide screen the tab is level with the far baseline, so there the linear meter's chip hangs 8 pt under the tab, kept to the meter's half 24 pt short of the centre line (`CoachPlace.hangs`); balanced lines, at most 280 pt wide and short of any tray plate level with it (up to 76 pt above its foot). It shows only in the player's own aim with no finger down, never with the nudge's ghost, and takes no touches. Drawing only: never fed to the engine, sent or recorded.

## Prior practice frame measurements

These Step D measurements used the original centred framing on the iPhone 17 simulator, iOS 26.2. A 120 Hz frame allows 8.33 ms. The Kit test advances a real dial throw in 1/120 s steps and times each step from the session call through interpolation, ground and piece rasterization and bitmap flush. The bitmap is 390 × 844 points at 2×. Release-time physics is measured separately. Timings are milliseconds.

## Reproduce locally

1. Run the usual Kit tests. Five fixed, bundled scenes cover fresh grass/classic, sand/USA, snow/Minnesota, mid-match/Wisconsin and plan/Sweden. Each 16 × 16 tile must have mean absolute RGB channel error below 4/255 against its native PNG. This permits antialiasing variation while protecting small kubbs. The projection test checks inversion and near-plane rejection on two aspect ratios.
2. The scripted practice test drives a second independent engine with the same input. It samples its camera and bodies throughout playback and compares SHA-256 of the canonical recording and settled view from each engine. The engine reserves its built-in match hash for match snapshots. The match golden suite still checks every golden action.
3. Run `node tools/render-review.mjs ../kubb.thingelstad.xyz` from the iOS worktree with the web dependencies and Chrome available. It rewrites the five synthetic JSON inputs here and generates web PNGs under `build/review/`. Playwright intercepts a loopback origin and serves renderer modules from the read-only web checkout. To export corresponding native PNGs, run `PITCH_RENDER_OUT="$PWD/build/review" swift test --package-path ThrowingWoodKit --filter RendererTests.snapshot`. Inspect the pictures before replacing any checked-in baseline. Join native first and web second horizontally for the comparison.
4. For simulator stills, launch the installed simulator build with `SIMCTL_CHILD_PITCH_REVIEW=aim`, `flight` or `rest` and `xcrun simctl launch --terminate-running-process <simulator> com.throwingwood.app`. Capture with `xcrun simctl io <simulator> screenshot <file.png>`. These modes snap to the next kubb and freeze the ordinary renderer at a reproducible tick. For display timings use `timing` and capture stdout. This mode plays the scripted release on the live display link and writes a single local timing summary. The review harness compiles only for Simulator.
5. For the frame-budget test use the Kit's simulator command with `-configuration Release ENABLE_TESTABILITY=YES -parallel-testing-enabled NO`. The local pitch server checks are in [ROOMS-CLIENT.md](ROOMS-CLIENT.md).


For the solo snapshots, build the simulator app with `xcodebuild -project ThrowingWood.xcodeproj -scheme ThrowingWood -configuration Release -destination 'generic/platform=iOS Simulator' -derivedDataPath build/dd CODE_SIGNING_ALLOWED=NO build` (the review set is a Release build; the review hooks are the simulator's, in either configuration). `tools/review-set.sh <group>` takes a group ("Retaking the review set" above). Run by hand, `python3 tools/solo-snapshots.py build/dd/Build/Products/Release-iphonesimulator/ThrowingWood.app` waits for each scene, captures to `build/solo-snapshots/` and checks every 24-point tile against the corresponding review PNG (mean RGB error below 4/255). The comparison masks the simulator-composited home indicator and Pro Max camera cutout. Use `--capture-only` to inspect a proposed new set before copying it, `--only` and `--skip` to pick shots, `--scene`, `--landscape`, `--ipad`, `--text-size accessibility-extra-extra-extra-large` (the `ax3` pictures) and `--player <name>` (the store's: a generated name for the fixtures' "You"). See [the session contract](MATCH.md) for checkpoint provenance.
