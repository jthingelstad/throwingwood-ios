# The iPhone pitch

Practice opens on grass. The surface picker changes the ground in the current session. Tap the pitch to put the circle there (a tap on or beside a standing kubb aims at it), or drag anywhere to move the circle by the finger's travel; lifting snaps it to a kubb it is near. Press the dial near six, wind back on the left, flick through six and lift on the right. Bringing the arm back slowly lets it down. The blue range is the release angle. The orange range is the backswing. Practice takes batons and the engine's `pointer` tune.

## Picture and clock

`KubbRenderer.PitchRenderer.draw(in:size:camera:scene:)` takes a top-left-origin Core Graphics context. Size is in points. The renderer saves and restores the caller's graphics state. Its only persistent geometry is the immutable, seeded ground texture and the six default stakes. A render never calls Lua.

- The camera is the engine's eye, orthonormal basis, focal length and principal point. In eye space, depth clips at 0.05 m. Projection divides right and up by depth, inverts screen y and applies the principal point. Landscape centres the 400 × 240 reference frame at `min(width/400, height/240)`. The portrait fit is described below. Sky and ground extend to every edge. The same transform casts drag points back onto z = 0.
- The scene accepts surface, set, posed items, advantage line, target, arc, thrower, raise footprints and punishment keep-out circles. The local z axis runs along each piece. Kubb, king, baton and stake extents, six stake positions, paint and team bands, light direction, hard shadows and near-plane clipping follow the web renderer. Nearby overlapping boxes use separating planes to correct depth order. The arc stops 0.45 m short of its target so the kubb stays visible.
- `PitchSession` owns the main-actor engine for practice and solo. A tap or target drag calls `setTarget` (`AimDrag`). A display tick consumes at most one pointer frame. `ThrowInput` isolates input sampling, lift and cancellation. The pointer implementation uses forward degrees from six, preserving touch boundaries and accumulating elapsed time between samples. Its stationary feed matches the web's stop detection. The display link sleeps on a still, unattended pitch and stops outside the active scene.
- The release frame already contains the applied throw. Decode its tracks, index moving bodies by id once, and retain static poses. Between samples, interpolate position and normalize the shortest-path quaternion blend. The session's one `showAt` call per moving frame supplies the camera, speed and dial. The typed playback response skips its redundant items array. The release tick can make both calls. Reaching rest holds the last picture for 0.8 s, then one `view` loads the next aim. Result feedback stays for six seconds into that aim.
- Timeline bursts follow world anchors. Flash, pop, the second KLAK, king crown and wood chips run on elapsed display time while the session can slow flight time. The engine supplies the flag and verdicts. Off-target words go first. The display face is Londrina Solid Black, with its OFL in the app bundle.

## Portrait framing

When height exceeds 1.05 times width, the scale is `1.3 × width / 400`. Frame y = 112 sits at 33% of screen height. The projected target gets 40 reference pixels of horizontal room; the pan is bounded by the cropped reference frame. The current pan applies immediately and survives release. The same fit projects the wood, target circle, burst anchors and plan, and casts touch points back onto the pitch. Human placement uses the web's separate still framing at 42% height, with 1.8× magnification for raising and 1× for punishment. These are host presentation transforms on the same cameras. The centred far base kubb on fresh grass measures **15.84 pt** on the 390 pt iPhone 16e and **17.87 pt** on the 440 pt iPhone 17 Pro Max, toe to top before the ink outline.

The dark stripe next to the former flight dial is the airborne baton's hard ground shadow. The renderer projects its shadow by (+0.55z, −0.30z) onto the ground. At tick 22 of the engine 0.5.0 practice throw, its centre in the original 390 × 844 framing is (317.44, 684.96) pt. A raster regression removes only the baton and measures the changed foreground pixels around that projection. The shadow stays, the dial and plan controls disappear during flight, and the pitch extends to the bottom.

## An iPad

Jamie: "Please take a moment to consider if now would be an okay time for us to build out an iPad version. We have our iPhone version, and I would definitely like to make one that is sized for iPad as well. I believe that our plate controls are well established and how the various landscape and portrait modes work. The idea of an iPad version would just be to give us a little bit bigger screen." So the app is universal and an iPad plays the same game on a bigger screen: no new controls, no other camera, no split views.

- **Orientations.** An iPad turns all four ways; an iPhone keeps portrait and landscape left and right. iPadOS 26 resizes every app's window (`UIRequiresFullScreen` is deprecated and ignored), so the app takes any window size and one scene (`UIApplicationSupportsMultipleScenes` false).
- **The same two rules.** A window taller than 1.05 times its width is laid out as the upright phone; any other is the web's wide arrangement above (`WideLayout`), both from the window's own size, never the device's. Upright the clock reaches its cap (radius 114) and the linear meter its 450 pt. On its side the clock is the web's S = min(0.28 W, 0.46 H, 380) (radius 137.7 pt on the iPad mini, 164.9 on the iPad Pro 13-inch) and the linear meter goes at its edge at 450 pt (track 378.3), standing on the bottom margin as before, so the rail and MENU keep their corners. The plan block is at the far edge, mirrored for Meter side Left; where the window is too narrow for the block and the minimap to keep 24 pt off the throwing line (wide windows about 610 pt across or less) the block goes back beside the clock, as on the web, but stays at the far edge beside the linear meter with the minimap over it or not drawn ("A phone on its side" above), so it never covers the prompt tab or the coach line. The raise and punishment bar is shown only in a wide window 460 pt high or less; taller windows keep the panel. Controls, plates and type keep their phone sizes; the pitch alone grows.
- **A window's controls.** In a window smaller than the screen iPadOS 26 puts close, minimise and full screen at the top leading corner and gives an app built for it no safe area for them. The app reads the safe area adapted to the window's corners (`UIView.edgeInsets(for: .safeArea(cornerAdaptation: .vertical))`) and adds its extra at the top to the root view controller's safe area (`WindowControls.swift`), so the rail and the cards sit clear of them (safe top 10 to 53 pt in a 700 × 486 window). Full screen and every iPhone add nothing.
- **Cards.** They keep the web's widths and stay centred: the score sheet min(640, 100%) (the web's `.sheet`), the tall raise and punishment panel 520 (`.panel`), Send feedback 560 (`.report`), the rest 330 as before. A phone keeps its sheet and feedback at full width, as before. A card taller than the window scrolls from its title (`FitOrScroll`), and the score sheet's notation starts at the top left of its box, not in its middle.
- **Turning or resizing mid-gesture** lets go as on a phone (`BoardView.layoutSubviews`, `PitchSession.cancelHeld`).

`WideLayoutTests` check both review iPads full screen, upright and on their side, both meters and both sides, and sweep window sizes (480 to 1400 pt wide and 380 to 1040 high, three sets of insets) for frames inside the safe area, nothing overlapping and the linear meter's room; `StillBarTests` and `ControlsTests` hold the bar and the raise touch on the iPads.

## Scenes

A scene (`PitchScene.look`: day, night, neon) changes only the picture and the controls' colours. The day's path in `PitchRenderer` is untouched: its five snapshots do not move. `SceneRenderer.swift` draws the night and the neon in the order of the web's `drawWorld` (sky, ground, lines, pieces, effects) with the web's numbers and colours, and `SceneID.shown` turns any id it does not know into the day.

- **Night**: a sky graded from 0x02040b to 0x16264a, a milky band, 5,200 steady and twinkling stars, a full moon with its halo, a moonlit clearing on the pitch in seven tones, the lines' glow and pieces lit from the moon with a glow round each. A throw leaves stardust behind the thrown piece (`SceneEffects`, the web's Stage.fx).
- **Neon**: black, a grid of light on the ground, a horizon band, pieces edged in light with coloured bands, a ribbon behind the thrown piece and sparks on a hit.
- **The skin**: `SceneSkin` holds the web's NIGHT.skin and NEON.skin for the plates, cards, sliders, STAND, both meters, the bursts and the HUD's plaques; the app reads it through `SkinStore`. `SceneSkinTests` checks every pair of words and ground at 7:1 or better. The day keeps the app's colours (its muted words stay the system's `.secondary`, and its red warn chip is as it was).
- **Reduce Motion**: no stardust trail and no ribbon, fewer and shorter puffs, no hit flash.
- **Glows** are the web's glow.js: one round sprite per colour, drawn once and stamped (`Glow.swift`). The geometry, colours and effect counts follow the web's code; the stars' exact pixels and the antialiasing are Core Graphics' own.

**Making it fast enough.** Core Graphics on iOS rasterizes on the CPU, after `draw(_:)` returns (`CABackingStoreUpdate` on the main thread), so the app's FrameTiming "update+draw" does not include it; the display callback's spacing does. Two things were slow and are now not: CG shades a linear gradient pixel by pixel, so the night sky and the neon's horizon band are filled as runs of whole device rows of one 8-bit colour (`rowGradient`); and thousands of tiny antialiased squares, so the steady stars are whole device pixels of about their own area, filled without antialiasing in one call. The picture is the same to the eye.

`PITCH_SCENE_TIMING=1 swift test -c release -Xswiftc -enable-testing --package-path ThrowingWoodKit --filter sceneDrawTiming` draws a practice throw at 3× (1170 × 2532) in each scene and prints the draw's p50, p95 and max. On the Mac (2026-11-20, ms, a busy machine):

| Scene | Before | After |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Day | 2.9 / 4.0 / 7 | 2.9 / 4.4 / 5.8 |
| Night | 38 / 48 / 52 | 16.5 / 25 / 39 |
| Neon | 13.2 / 25 / 30 | 11 / 20 / 24 |

On the iPhone 17 simulator (iOS 26.2, Debug, about 60 Hz, `PITCH_REVIEW=timing`) a throw had 154 display callbacks 16.67 ms apart at the median in the day, 128 to 141 at 17.2 to 17.8 ms in the night (p95 23 to 33; 79 to 85 at 29 to 33 ms before), and 143 to 145 at 16.67 ms in the neon (p95 about 32). What the night still spends is its look: the milky band's 28 big glows (about 4.5 ms, up to 9 looking up), the moon's halo (1 to 4), the clearing's seven tones (about 5) and the lines' glow (about 2.7). Each could be thinned (fewer or smaller glows, a smaller halo, fewer tones, a lower-resolution sky); none has been. A physical phone's numbers are still to measure.

The review tools take `--scene night` or `neon` (solo: `REVIEW_SCENE` picks the options' Scene; online: the host picks it in the lobby) and name the pictures `<phone>-<scene>[-landscape]-<shot>.png`, e.g. [night, aim](review/iphone-16e-night-aim.png), [night on its side](review/iphone-17-pro-max-night-landscape-flight.png), [neon, the winner](review/iphone-17-pro-max-neon-winner.png), [neon on an iPad](review/ipad-pro-13-neon-landscape-aim.png), [the night lobby as host](review/online/iphone-16e-night-host.png) and [the options' Scene row](review/iphone-16e-options.png).

### Drawing details

- Quartz rasterizes the ink edges and dashes. Grass blades use short batches of filled, hard-edged shapes and sand/snow specks have square ends. Their positions still use the web seed. The playfield and dial use at most two backing pixels per point for the moving-frame budget. SwiftUI controls and text use display resolution.
- Batons have 24-sided circular cross-sections with a projected cap and highlight, plain wood in every set with no bands (Jamie, 2026-11-20: "Batons are always plain and never changed for the set."). The web constructs analytic ellipses. End-grain rings and pith, crown interiors, cheese spots and some bevels use simpler paths. Small differences appear on close wood and around silhouettes. These are presentation approximations. Positions in moving pieces use the recording's 1e−5 quantization; the session camera uses its full-precision playback.
- The provisional dial uses the web's angle convention, zones, prompt, power and release targets and last-throw marks. It has a simpler face, hand, ticks and knob. The native burst uses its own text metrics, short chip animation and crown path. Labels use the system face, display words use Londrina. The native screen stacks its verdict under the heading. These UI differences are intentional.

## The meter

The throw's meter is the player's choice, kept on the device: MENU (practice) or the match menus, Meter, Clock or Linear (`pitch.meter`, clock by default), and Side, Left or Right (`pitch.meterSide`, right by default) for the linear meter. `KubbRenderer/ThrowMeter.swift` holds the geometry and which touches each meter claims, and both feed the same `PointerDial`: the clock its finger's angle about the centre, the linear meter `meterPoint(arm:)` for the knob's height. The engine and its `pointer` tune do not know which meter is on the screen.

- **The clock** keeps its place and size: radius `min(0.22 W, 0.12 H, 114)` pt, centre 42 pt above the bottom safe area. Its colours are the app's own (system red and grey), not the web's palette.
- **The linear meter** (`LinearMeterHUD.swift`) is a port of the web's `src/render/linear.js`, its tune number for number: two columns in one ink frame with a hard shadow, the lane (targets) toward the screen's centre and the channel (the wood knob) toward the edge, the prompt a tab at the knob's home. The knob's home is the clock's 6, the middle of the track; the backswing runs down, toward the player, and the forward swing up, on one scale. The box is 0.2 of the width (64 to 96 pt) and 0.47 of the height, at least 390 pt and at most 450 pt, the web's, and stands 7 pt off the edge and the bottom safe area, under the minimap. An upright screen that cannot give it 320 pt of track (a short phone) shows the clock. On a wide screen the box has the whole safe height and needs 260 pt of track ("A phone on its side" above). Its words: DRAW BACK, FLING!, BACK TO MIDDLE. The plan's sliders and buttons are padded clear of it through the `\.meterRoom` environment value (`MeterRoom`: the edge and the width the meter takes). With the meter on the left the plan's two columns swap, TILT or SPIN over STAND beside the meter and ARC over SIDE VIEW at the far edge, so the shorter column sits under the prompt tab and the tall ARC never covers it. STAND's body is 48 pt (the web's is about 44 px) so TILT over it stays under the tab. The sliders are SwiftUI views over the board, so a press on one never reaches `BoardView` (never a throw), and a press on the meter never reaches a slider.
- **Red** starts at `max(overDeg, powerHi)` on both meters: the engine's overpower cost counts from the power target's top (core/throwplan), so a long throw's target past 11 o'clock is never drawn as overpowered.
- **The last throw** (the engine's `lastBack` and `lastStop`) is a faded ghost of the knob where it was, 60% of its size at 45% opacity, so it reads as "where your last throw went" and never as a target. The web draws the same.

### Touches

In `BoardView.touchesBegan`, in this order. Anything a step does not claim falls to the next.

1. Raise and punishment phases: the raise is a touch on the kubb (a tap, a tap by an end's number, or a drag that tips it; docs/MATCH.md), punishment as before.
2. `MeterLayout.first`: the clock's ring, within 1.25 radii of its centre (the throw at once, as always); the linear knob, 46 pt up or down from home, across the body and 6 pt either side of it (the throw at once, grabbed relatively: the press point is home); the rest of the linear body and its shadow (swallowed, never a throw). On a wide screen the knob grabs across its channel only (6 pt past the body's outer edge), and the rest of the body is throw zone (`.zone`: a tap aims at what is behind it, a drag strokes), since there it stands over the far baseline; `MeterLayout.press` says what a claim comes to once the press lifts or moves.
3. The thrower's stance mark (44 pt), dragged directly. STAND is a SwiftUI plate in the plan and never reaches `BoardView`.
4. `MeterLayout.after`: beside the clock's ring, 1.25 to 2.1 radii; the linear meter's throw zone, y ≥ 0.6 of the height upright (`zoneFrom`), and on a wide screen the meter's half, top to bottom: x ≥ 0.5 W with the meter at the right, x ≤ 0.5 W at the left (`wideZone`). The lower 40% of a phone on its side is about 156 pt, too shallow for a whole draw back and flick; the meter's half gives the thumb the screen's height, and the plan block and the minimap are on the other half, where a drag still moves the aim. Beside the ring where a tap could not aim (`tapCouldAim`, the tap rule `target(tap: true)` uses), the press is the throw at once. Otherwise the press waits: one that moves more than 10 pt (`MeterLayout.tapSlop`) is the throw, a stroke from the knob's home for the zone, and one that does not is a tap on the pitch.
5. The aim (`AimDrag`): a tap puts the circle there, a drag anywhere else moves it from where it is. A tap that waited in step 4 aims the same way (`tapAim`).

A stroke from the zone or beside the ring feeds the engine the press on one frame and the finger on the next, so a quick first move still arms the swing. A finger beside the ring is drawn as a spoke from the knob to it.

### The nudge

When a player is stuck on their own throw, a ghost of the knob makes one good throw on the meter with a ghost fingertip where the finger goes: a slow draw back (0.9 s), a beat, a fast flick (0.25 s), then a second's rest, until the next press. `ThrowNudge.swift` (pure, the web's `src/game/nudge.js` numbers) says when and where on the timeline; `NudgeHUD.swift` draws it. It counts only eligible time (the player's own aim in practice or a match, no finger down, the side view closed and no slider held, the dial idle; a held slider counts as input) on the wall clock, and the display link sleeps until it is due. A device with fewer than 3 throws (`pitch.throwsMade`, capped at 9) is nudged after 8 s in the aim without a press on the meter; any device after two presses on the meter in a row that made no throw (0.8 s after the last); a learned one after 30 s with no input. A tap in the throw zone or beside the ring keeps it away 1.5 s. The fingertip is beside the ring (1.6 radii) on the clock in practice; in a match the plan's sliders sit beside the ring, so it presses the knob instead. On the linear meter it is in the throw zone on the meter's inner side, between the two slider columns; on a wide screen it is in the meter's half, halfway from the centre line to the prompt tab, level with the knob's home. While the engine says WIND BACK the plate follows the ghost. Reduce Motion shows a still picture at the back with the strokes' arrows. Drawing only: the ghost is never fed to the engine.

### The coach line

While a device is learning (fewer than 3 throws, `pitch.throwsMade`) a few words sit just over the meter's prompt and follow the stroke: PULL BACK TO THE ORANGE MARK at rest, NO RUSH GOING BACK while drawing back short of the power band, NOW FLICK FAST AND STOP ON THE BLUE MARK from the band through the flick. After a throw of this player's own that the engine called SLOW, the next throw is coached on any device, led by SLOW FLICK. FLICK FASTER. in an ink box. `KubbRenderer/Coach.swift` is the web's `src/game/coach.js` (its tests ported in `CoachTests`); `CoachHUD.swift` draws it as the web's hud.js `showCoach`: over the clock's plate, centred, or from the linear meter's inner edge over its tab. On a wide screen the tab is level with the far baseline, so there the linear meter's line hangs 8 pt under the tab, the lead over the line, both kept to the meter's half 24 pt short of the centre line (`CoachPlace.hangs`); 13 pt heavy, upper case, balanced lines, at most 280 pt wide and short of any plan column level with it (up to 76 pt above its foot, read from the columns' frames). It shows only in the player's own aim with no finger down, never with the nudge's ghost, and takes no touches. The face is the system's heavy font, not the web's CSS face. Drawing only: never fed to the engine, sent or recorded.

`python3 tools/meter-snapshots.py <simulator-app> --device <name or udid>` captures every meter, side, mark and nudge frame under `build/meter-shots/` (`PITCH_REVIEW` modes `wind`, `beside`, `marks` and `nudge`; `NUDGE_AT` freezes the nudge at a time, `NUDGE_STILL` shows the still picture, `THROWS_MADE` sets `pitch.throwsMade` for the coach, 9 by default). The review tools launch with `-pitch.throwsMade 9` so the coach stays away, and 0 for the coach shots. `--landscape left` or `right` on the solo, meter and online tools turns the simulator's interface on its side (`REVIEW_ORIENTATION`, simulator only; the board writes its ready file only once the window has turned) and names the pictures `<phone>-landscape-<shot>.png`. ## Prior practice frame measurements

These Step D measurements used the original centred framing on the iPhone 17 simulator, iOS 26.2. A 120 Hz frame allows 8.33 ms. The Kit test advances a real dial throw in 1/120 s steps and times each step from the session call through interpolation, ground and piece rasterization and bitmap flush. The bitmap is 390 × 844 points at 2×. Release-time physics is measured separately. Timings are milliseconds.

## Reproduce locally

1. Run the usual Kit tests. Five fixed, bundled scenes cover fresh grass/classic, sand/USA, snow/Minnesota, mid-match/Wisconsin and plan/Sweden. Each 16 × 16 tile must have mean absolute RGB channel error below 4/255 against its native PNG. This permits antialiasing variation while protecting small kubbs. The projection test checks inversion and near-plane rejection on two aspect ratios.
2. The scripted practice test drives a second independent engine with the same input. It samples its camera and bodies throughout playback and compares SHA-256 of the canonical recording and settled view from each engine. The engine reserves its built-in match hash for match snapshots. The match golden suite still checks every golden action.
3. Run `node tools/render-review.mjs ../kubb.thingelstad.xyz` from the iOS worktree with the web dependencies and Chrome available. It rewrites the five synthetic JSON inputs here and generates web PNGs under `build/review/`. Playwright intercepts a loopback origin and serves renderer modules from the read-only web checkout. To export corresponding native PNGs, run `PITCH_RENDER_OUT="$PWD/build/review" swift test --package-path ThrowingWoodKit --filter RendererTests.snapshot`. Inspect the pictures before replacing any checked-in baseline. Join native first and web second horizontally for the comparison.
4. For simulator stills, launch the installed simulator build with `SIMCTL_CHILD_PITCH_REVIEW=aim`, `flight` or `rest` and `xcrun simctl launch --terminate-running-process <simulator> com.throwingwood.app`. Capture with `xcrun simctl io <simulator> screenshot <file.png>`. These modes snap to the next kubb and freeze the ordinary renderer at a reproducible tick. For display timings use `timing` and capture stdout. This mode plays the scripted release on the live display link and writes a single local timing summary. The review harness compiles only for Simulator.
5. For the frame-budget test use the Kit's simulator command with `-configuration Release ENABLE_TESTABILITY=YES -parallel-testing-enabled NO`. Local room conformance and cross-play commands are in [ROOMS-CLIENT.md](ROOMS-CLIENT.md).


For the solo snapshots, build the simulator app with `xcodebuild -project ThrowingWood.xcodeproj -scheme ThrowingWood -destination 'generic/platform=iOS Simulator' -derivedDataPath build/dd CODE_SIGNING_ALLOWED=NO build`. Run `python3 tools/solo-snapshots.py build/dd/Build/Products/Debug-iphonesimulator/ThrowingWood.app`. The script waits for each scene, captures to `build/solo-snapshots/` and checks every 24-point tile against the corresponding review PNG (mean RGB error below 4/255). The comparison masks the simulator-composited home indicator and Pro Max camera cutout. Use `--capture-only` to inspect a proposed new set before copying it. See [the session contract](MATCH.md) for checkpoint provenance.
