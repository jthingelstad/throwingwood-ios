# The offline solo match

The home screen opens practice or a match against the computer. PLAY THE COMPUTER resumes an unfinished solo game or opens its options. START creates a new match. REMATCH returns to the options. The match menu pauses the clock and provides level, Raising, Meter (Clock or Linear, and Side for the linear one), score sheet, home and resignation.

## Session contract

- `PitchSession` owns one main-actor Lua state. The app gets the six selectors from `room.constants`. It uses LEVELS for level and MATCH_OPTIONS and OPTION_DEFAULTS for the other five. Club is the web solo default. The host supplies the local Gregorian start date, a random 31-bit seed and a name for each side. Side A plays first and side B is the computer.
- On `new` and `restore`, set control to sides A and CPU true. Adopt the session's `view`. A throw need supplies the thrower, projectile, position in the turn and standing counts. A human handoff pauses until THROW. Lua handles the computer's own raises and handovers. A CPU throw advances through ordinary `frame({dt})` calls including its think and windup.
- While aiming, the pointer dial supplies arm angles. A tap places the target where the finger lands, or on a standing kubb or king whose drawn body is under or within 22 pt of the tap. A drag anywhere on the pitch moves the circle from where it is by the finger's travel, 0.01 m a point along the camera's right and forward laid on the ground, with `setTarget` and no snap; lifting sends the same point once more with the engine's snap. Drag the stance mark directly or enable WALK and drag to walk. After an aim command, read the session's dial so a stationary player sees the updated release ranges before winding back. The session constrains all movement. The minimap tracks the thrower's frame on either side.
- The plan. ARC is a vertical slider over the engine's release range (15 to 165 degrees, `setArc`); a press anywhere on it jumps the knob there and keeps dragging, and the value is sent at most once a display frame. Its header shows the loft (`ARC 46°`), its marks the clock's hours with a long one at the level throw (90). TILT for a baton (5 degrees a step to 20 either way) or SPIN for a kubb (1 a step to 3) is a horizontal slider: the knob follows the finger and settles on the nearest step, sent as the step difference through `stepSide`; the header reads `TILT 10R`, `SPIN 2L` or `TILT 0`. VoiceOver adjusts either one step at a time (`stepArc`, `stepSide`). SIDE VIEW (once PLAN VIEW) toggles the engine's side camera, and the same camera shows while either slider is held and 0.5 s after. The plan's row keeps clear of the linear meter (`\.meterRoom`, docs/RENDER.md "The meter"); with the meter on the left the two columns swap so TILT sits beside it. While a slider is held, a one-line plaque under the turn rail says what it does, and TILT draws the baton leaning by the tilt over the circle as the thrower sees it. Nothing else reaches the engine.
- A release has already advanced the match. Save the raw snapshot, play the recording and keep the releasing side's HUD until rest. The controller gets camera and speed from `showAt` and interpolates the recording's poses. SKIP enters the settled view. Rulings, bursts and verdict words come from the engine.
- For `need.kind == raise`, draw the lying kubb and the session's numbered end footprints. The player touches the kubb (`RaiseTouch.swift`, the web's raise-touch.js numbers): a tap on it, or within 24 pt, stands it on the nearer end; a tap near an end's number takes that end; a drag tips it toward the end the finger moves to, drawn with the engine's tip pose, and stands it past half way (short of that nothing is sent). A refused end is never sent: the allowed end lights for a second instead. END 1 and END 2 are VoiceOver actions naming the left and right ends. Match the selected end's `which` to the one-based index in `choices`. An automatic raise omits the index. AUTO raises this kubb by the engine's choice; AUTO ALL, shown while more than one kubb is still to raise, raises the rest of this turn's 0.7 s apart. Raising is the player's choice for the rest of this match, as on the web (auto-raise.js): REST OF GAME on the panel (shown after AUTO ALL, as on the web) and the menu's Raising row (By hand or Automatic, shown once the match has asked this device to raise) do the same for every raise of this device's side; the card says so and offers BY HAND. Nothing is kept on the device. Automatic raising never acts for the other side or under a card, and stops (leaving the kubb to the player) if the engine refuses. For punishment the touch ray meets the ground, the cursor stays within the supplied bounds, and `checkPunish` decides whether PLACE IT is available. Both use `apply` and load the resulting view.
- An over need shows the winner even if a resignation retained a previous handoff in the session. The end card takes its winner, reason and statistics from `need`. The sheet displays `sheet().shown` by default. Show positions, COPY and SAVE use the complete Planet Kubb Notation text returned by the session. SAVE uses the session's filename and the iOS file picker.
- The app writes the opaque UTF-8 snapshot atomically in Application Support at match start and after each committed action. A cold launch restores the session and reestablishes control. During flight the committed throw is already in that file. Cold restoration opens at the next decision. An ordinary background/foreground cycle preserves the in-memory camera and pauses display time. Home keeps an unfinished save for the next PLAY THE COMPUTER. Home after a win clears the finished save.

## Send feedback

Practice and solo both offer SEND FEEDBACK in the menu (the web's src/ui/report.js).
The card asks what it is about: "A throw looked wrong" (first when the game has a
last throw) or "Something else". A throw report has the two answers and the pieces
to mark: the player can tap several pieces on the overhead pitch or choose their
names when pieces overlap, and the card freezes the last throw's rest positions,
including pieces collected at the end of a turn. Something else is one box of up to
2000 characters. Both send the game as it stood when the card opened, with no name or
contact field. The wire is the web's: `kind: "throw"` with the answers, selected IDs
and poses, or `kind: "feedback"` with a trimmed `text` and no answers; both carry the
replay state. A failed send keeps the draft open. Resuming a solo save starts its
report history at that checkpoint.
When practice history exceeds the report size limit, the report keeps the engine's
pre-throw checkpoint and the action. Replaying the checkpoint restores the surface,
fallen kubbs and previously thrown batons with their full-precision poses.

## Differences from browser solo

Both clients use the same pointer tune, options, computer, rules, raises, punishments and notation. Native controls use a paused match menu, native option pickers, a compact turn rail with batons left and both sides' standing counts, numbered end labels and a smaller schematic minimap. The winner shows hits as a fraction. The sheet exports through Files. The touch name field counts extended grapheme clusters. The browser counts UTF-16 code units.

The app hides the dial in flight, keeps the pitch full height and applies the target pan immediately. The current browser source still draws a promptless dial in playback and eases its aim pan. Quartz wood, ink, chips and text metrics differ slightly from Canvas. Saving and resuming a private match on the phone is deliberate. The sound and haptic cues share the playback clock. See [Pitch feedback](FEEL.md). Browser links to rules and the notation guide, full how-to overlays, keyboard/mouse shortcuts and web release controls are outside the native screen. Simulator timing, physical touch comfort and VoiceOver pitch manipulation still need device evaluation.

## Verification

`SoloTests` plays an uncapped, seeded computer match on both sides through the production controller and a separate engine. It compares the hash at every release and the finished sheet. It also restores an atomic mid-flight save, checks the next release, tests projected legal end selection and punishment refusal/placement, and exercises the actual option, plan and stance calls. `ControlsTests` covers the slider arithmetic (the release range, the side steps, one `setArc` a frame, the step difference), the aim drag's scale and axes, tap-to-target, and automatic raising: its gap, every kubb stood by the engine's choice with the hash checked against a second engine, and no raise for the other side or under a card.

The five raw simulator checkpoints in `ThrowingWoodKit/Tests/KubbRendererTests/Fixtures/solo/` come only from a synthetic match. Regenerate them with `node tools/solo-fixtures.mjs <web-checkout>` on engine 0.4.0. A second engine plans the scripted human's throws. Every checkpoint is the original match's untouched snapshot. The fixtures are test resources. Simulator review reads one copied into the app's temporary directory. The installed device app only reads its own local save. `raise-many.json` (two kubbs to raise for the human side) comes from the same synthetic recipe in Swift, seed 1001: `TW_WRITE_RAISE_FIXTURE=1 swift test --package-path ThrowingWoodKit --filter writeRaiseManyFixture`. `raise-refused.json` (a raise whose first end the engine refuses, invented teams, pass and play) comes from the engine's own seeded match: `TW_WRITE_RAISE_FIXTURE=refused swift test --package-path ThrowingWoodKit --filter writeRaiseRefusedFixture`.
