# The offline solo match

The home screen opens practice, a match against the computer and Settings. PLAY THE COMPUTER resumes an unfinished solo game or opens its options. START creates a new match. REMATCH returns to the options. The options card (Play the computer, the 1.0 design) shows the player's name in its name row (a generated name and SHUFFLE: AGENTS.md "Names are generated"), then the rows of the engine's card for the chosen scene (`pitch.optionRows({ scene })`, engine 0.14; `SoloOptions.cardRows`): Scene as seven picture tiles drawn by the app's renderer from a low postcard camera (Park, Beach, Camping, Techno, Minnesota, Tournament, Black & White, the engine's names), Surface under it only where the scene leaves one (Black & White: grass, sand, snow), then Computer (Kid, Beginner, Club, Pro), Opening, Raising and Wind as segmented bars, HOME quiet and START go. The tiles wrap as the web's do: three a row on a screen under 600 pt wide (the seventh centred on a row of its own), four from 600; their names in the buttons' Londrina Solid at 16 (the web's Inter 14 does not fit TOURNAMENT in a third of the 16e's card); at the accessibility text sizes each is a row, its picture beside its name. There is no Kubbs row: the scene decides the set (Minnesota's own, the Tournament's USA, Classic elsewhere). Choosing a scene takes its card's defaults; starting, keeping and loading the selections go through the scene (`SoloOptions.starting`), so a surface or set kept from before that the scene does not allow comes back as the scene's. The player has no level of their own to set: from engine 0.13 a person throws on one meter (AGENTS.md "Levels are the computer's"). The practice pitch's MENU opens a card of its own (`PracticeMenu`): SETTINGS, HOW TO THROW and FEEDBACK, then the practice's Scene (the seven tiles) and its Surface only where the scene leaves one (a segmented bar), BACK TO THE PITCH go and HOME quiet. The match menu pauses the clock: a two by two grid of SETTINGS (the device's, below), SCORE SHEET, HOW TO THROW and FEEDBACK, Raising (this match's own), BACK TO THE MATCH, and RESIGN and HOME quiet.

Where a setting lives follows its scope (the owner's sheet, 2026-11-20). The device's, Your name (`PlayerName`, `pitch.name`, a generated name also picked in a set-up screen's You row), Sound, Haptics, Meter, Meter side and App icon (the home screen's icon, a tile per scene's king, kept by iOS: docs/RENDER.md "The app icon"), are together on the Settings card, reached from home, a set-up screen's line, the match and pitch menus and the practice menu. The game's are the match options only: the solo options card, the practice menu (Surface, kept as `pitch.practiceSurface` as the web keeps `practiceSurface`, and Scene) and the lobby. A turn's and a throw's settings are on the throw controls, and the engine keeps them for their scope (SPIN and STAND for the turn; ARC and TILT back at each throw). `SettingsScopesTests` checks where each is presented. Before 0.5 the solo card kept its own name (`solo.name`); it is dropped unread now, and a typed `pitch.name` is replaced by a rolled name on load.

A finished match against the computer can be shared and given a highlight reel from its winner card: MAKE A HIGHLIGHT REEL opens the share card, and after a share the same button opens the reel card for the returned code. Nothing is sent before SHARE AND MAKE THE REEL. The card reads the kept finished match (`MatchSave.finishedMatch()`, below) only when it is the match on the screen. docs/ONLINE.md "Sharing a match against the computer".

The phone can be held on its side as well as upright (Jamie, 2026-11-19: "Given we have a widescreen view for web, can we extend that to support iPhone rotated display?"). On its side the match takes the web's wide arrangement: the rail top left, MENU top right, the clock low in the Meter side corner, the set-up tray at the other edge, the minimap under the header's right end. The clock is always shown there, because no phone on its side has the linear meter's room. The match, its save and the engine do not know which way the phone is held. Turning it mid-gesture lets go of the dial, an aim drag, a slider or STAND with nothing sent. docs/RENDER.md "A phone on its side".

## Session contract

- `PitchSession` owns one main-actor Lua state. The app gets the six selectors from `pitch.constants`. It uses MATCH_OPTIONS and OPTION_DEFAULTS; on engine 0.9 they include Computer, the computer's level, which `startSolo` passes as the match's `computer` option (the computer's player entry names no level). An older engine has no such row, so the app makes one from LEVELS and puts its value on the computer's entry, as before 0.9. Club is the web solo default. Side A's player entry names no level: from engine 0.13 a person throws on one meter (AGENTS.md "Levels are the computer's"); the card's Computer level row is side B's, kept per game in `solo.option.computer` (read from `solo.option.level` once). The card's first row is Scene (the engine's seven, `SceneID.catalogue` the app's copy), kept like the others as `solo.option.scene`; `startSolo` passes it as the match's `scene` option with the surface and set it resolves to (the engine resolves them again: since 0.14 a named scene decides), the pitch behind the card shows the pick, and the match, its reopening after a cold launch, its replay, its winner card and its share (the match's start carries it) are drawn in it. An older engine has no Scene row and its match is the day. A restored match keeps the scene it has; an id this build does not draw is the day and is kept untouched. Practice takes the scene of its menu's Scene row (`pitch.practiceScene`), always sent with `new`; a change starts a new practice in it on the same surface where the scene allows it, else the scene's own.

A Kid match (engine 0.15, kubb-engine docs/notes/kid-mode.md) is the Kid computer: Kid is first on the Computer row and a kept `computer: "kid"` loads. The engine does the rest (three base kubbs a side, every kubb and every baton at a kubb from the Kid line 2 m in front of the baseline, the king shot from the baseline, an early king stood back up, no punishment kubbs, helicopters count, the Kid meter). The app draws the Kid line (`aim.kidY`: dots and two cones, docs/RENDER.md), words STAND for it ("Stand anywhere from behind your line up to the Kid line.", "ON THE KID LINE"), puts "Kid match · throw from the cones" (or "· the king from your line", or plain "Kid match") over the hand-off card's title from `need.kid` (`HandoffWords.kid`, the web's KID_LINES), and shows the ruling's flag where a Kid ruling says it better (`ThrowResult.shownFlag`, the web's flagFor): KING BACK UP, OUT TWICE: PLACED, and what a counted helicopter knocked down, never HELICOPTER. Nothing the app says tells a child they were punished or lost. The host supplies the local Gregorian start date, a random 31-bit seed and a name for each side. Side A plays first and side B is the computer.
- On `new` and `restore`, set control to sides A and CPU true. Adopt the session's `view`. A throw need supplies the thrower, projectile, position in the turn and standing counts. A human handoff pauses until THROW: a short card at the foot of the screen (the 1.0 design), "Your turn" (or the team's), one line of what the thrower faces (`HandoffWords.line`: "Six batons. Two field kubbs to clear first.", "Six batons. Aim for the base kubbs.", "Six batons. Go for the king.", "Throw three kubbs into the other half.", from the engine's rail) and THROW. The computer's turn has no card: its throws show the WATCH tab on Team B's colour over the faded meter. Lua handles the computer's own raises and handovers. A CPU throw advances through ordinary `frame({dt})` calls including its think and windup.
- While aiming, the pointer dial supplies arm angles. A tap places the target where the finger lands, or on a standing kubb or king whose drawn body is under or within 22 pt of the tap. A drag anywhere on the pitch moves the circle from where it is by the finger's travel, 0.01 m a point along the camera's right and forward laid on the ground, with `setTarget` and no snap; lifting sends the same point once more with the engine's snap. Drag the stance mark directly, or use STAND in the plan (below). After an aim command, read the session's dial so a stationary player sees the updated release ranges before winding back. The session constrains all movement. The minimap tracks the thrower's frame on either side.
- The plan: the set-up tray (the 1.0 design, stage 3; `TrayLayout.swift`, `TrayView`, docs/RENDER.md). ARC, TILT (SPIN for a kubb) and STAND are three closed plates of one size in a column, each its value over a small picture of it (the arc as high as the loft, the baton leaning or the kubb turning, the ring on the line); VoiceOver adjusts each a step at a time from the closed plate. A tap opens one (`tapPlate`, one at a time) into the control below; ARC open shows the side view until it closes, and a tap outside, on its band or a press on the meter closes it. ARC is a vertical slider over the engine's release range (15 to 165 degrees, `setArc`); a press anywhere on it jumps the knob there and keeps dragging, and the value is sent at most once a display frame. Its header shows the loft (`ARC 46°`), its marks the clock's hours with a long one at the level throw (90). TILT for a baton (-20 to 20 degrees) or SPIN for a kubb (-3 to 3 taps) is a horizontal slider. On engine 0.9 each slider takes its numbers from the engine (`view.aim.controls`: range, value, the plan's own, `tick` and `step`; Jamie, 2026-11-20: "more analog allow the slider to select any value in a range") and sends where the finger is, any value, through `setArc`, `setTilt` or `setSpin`; its marks are a `tick` apart, or the fewest ticks apart that divide a step and leave 4 pt between marks (tilt every 5 degrees, spin every half tap, ARC every 15 degrees on today's tracks), and the value is free between them. The header reads `TILT 6R` (whole degrees), `SPIN 1.25L` (to a hundredth) or `TILT 0`. On a 0.8 match (no controls) the side settles on the nearest step and is sent as the step difference through `stepSide`, ARC's marks are the clock's hours, as before 0.9. VoiceOver adjusts either one step at a time (`stepArc`, `stepSide`). Arc and tilt go back to the plan's own at every throw; a kubb's spin is kept for the side's next kubb in the inkast turn (the engine's `keptSpin`, scope `turn`), and the slider shows it: the app never resets it. STAND (`StandPad.swift`, the web's stand-pad.js, which replaced WALK, the Playdate's held A) is the throwing line seen from above: the ground behind it the rules allow, the advantage line when a baton may stand up to it, and a ring where the thrower stands. A press or drag puts the ring under the finger through `setStance`, sent at most once a frame; the plate's ground is fixed at the press so it never shifts under the finger. While it is held and a second after, the plaque says where in words (`1.5 M RIGHT, 1.7 M BACK. STAND ON YOUR LINE OR BEHIND IT.`). VoiceOver adjusts it 0.25 m along the line, with step actions for all four ways. SIDE VIEW (once PLAN VIEW) is no longer a button: ARC open is the engine's side camera, and the same camera shows while either slider is held and 0.5 s after. The tray stands at the edge away from the meter. While a slider is held, a one-line plaque under the turn rail says what it does, and TILT draws the baton leaning by the tilt over the circle as the thrower sees it. Nothing else reaches the engine.
- A release has already advanced the match. Save the raw snapshot, play the recording and keep the releasing side's HUD until rest. The controller gets camera and speed from `showAt` and interpolates the recording's poses. SKIP enters the settled view. Rulings, bursts and verdict words come from the engine.
- For `need.kind == raise`, draw the lying kubb and the session's numbered end footprints. The player touches the kubb (`RaiseTouch.swift`, the web's raise-touch.js numbers): a tap on it, or within 24 pt, stands it on the nearer end; a tap near an end's number takes that end; a drag tips it toward the end the finger moves to, drawn with the engine's tip pose, and stands it past half way (short of that nothing is sent). A refused end is never sent: the allowed end lights for a second instead. END 1 and END 2 are VoiceOver actions naming the left and right ends. Match the selected end's `which` to the one-based index in `choices`. An automatic raise omits the index. AUTO raises this kubb by the engine's choice; AUTO ALL, shown while more than one kubb is still to raise, raises the rest of this turn's. Every automatic raise is watched (the owner, 2026-11-22: "When you hit auto to raise kubbs you should watch the computer raise them."; `RaiseShow` in AutoRaise.swift, the web's lift.js): in the raise scene the first kubb of the turn's automatic raise lies 0.4 s (`Pacing.autoRaiseWait`, 0.7 for every kubb before), then each kubb tips up onto the end the engine chose (the need's `default` choice) with the same tip the hand drag draws, eased, over 70% of its beat, and stands for the rest (a kubb a crowded raise moved slides to its spot in half of it); the beat is half a second, shorter when there are many so the whole raise takes four seconds at most (`Pacing.raiseBeat`: min(0.5, 3.6 / kubbs), never under 0.18). Its `raise` action, the same as before and in the same order, is applied and sent as its kubb starts to tip (AUTO tips at once: the player is already looking at it). A tap on the pitch skips to the end: this kubb's action goes at once and the rest of this turn's automatic raise follows with no wait and no tip, one action a frame (online, each as soon as the last has landed). The computer's own raises and every raise of a match whose Raising is Automatic have no raise need on this device (the engine stands them as the turn changes), so they are watched after the throw that sent the kubbs over (`StoodShow`): each kubb that lay and now stands within a metre of where it lay tips up in the raise scene, one after another at the same pace, 0.4 s first, before the next view; a kubb put somewhere else (a punishment kubb the engine placed) just appears. An online pitch's other members and observers watch each arriving `raise` tip up with no lying first (`PitchSession.watchRaise`); OnlinePitch draws the next action only after it, and each action waiting behind it makes it run two times faster more (`RaisePace.catchUp`), so they never fall behind. Under Reduce Motion there is no tip: each kubb lies for the first half of its beat and stands for the second, one at a time. VoiceOver hears one line for the whole of it ("Raising 4 kubbs automatically.", "Computer stood 3 kubbs up."), never a line a kubb. Raising is the player's choice for the rest of this match, as on the web (auto-raise.js): REST OF GAME on the panel (shown after AUTO ALL, as on the web) and the menu's Raising row (By hand or Automatic, shown once the match has asked this device to raise) do the same for every raise of this device's side; the card says so and offers BY HAND. Nothing is kept on the device. Automatic raising never acts for the other side or under a card, and stops (leaving the kubb to the player) if the engine refuses. For punishment the touch ray meets the ground, the cursor stays within the supplied bounds, and `checkPunish` decides whether PLACE IT is available. Both use `apply` and load the resulting view.
- The last throw's verdict never stops a player aiming (the owner, 2026-11-22: "the display stays on the screen for a while keeping the player from being able to aim at times"): it takes no touches, goes at the first press of the aim (on the pitch, the target, the stance, the meter, a tray plate, a slider or STAND; `PitchSession.verdictAway`), and untouched stays `Pacing.verdictShown`, 3.5 s from the throw coming to rest (6 s before, counted from the next aim), the web's flagFor. It stands in the band under the header, left of the minimap, and stops 8 pt above the top of the target ring, a kubb in it and the arc's last 1.5 m (`WideLayout.verdictBand`, `verdictClear`; on its side the caption room is stopped the same way), giving way as the caption stack does where that is short. VoiceOver says it once when it comes.
- An over need shows the winner even if a resignation retained a previous handoff in the session. The end card takes its winner, reason and statistics from `need`. The sheet displays `sheet().shown` by default. Show positions, COPY and SAVE use the complete Planet Kubb Notation text returned by the session. SAVE uses the session's filename and the iOS file picker.
- The app writes the opaque UTF-8 snapshot atomically in Application Support at match start and after each committed action. A cold launch restores the session and reestablishes control. During flight the committed throw is already in that file. Cold restoration opens at the next decision. An ordinary background/foreground cycle preserves the in-memory camera and pauses display time. Home keeps an unfinished save for the next PLAY THE COMPUTER. Home after a win clears the finished save.

## The replay trail

A match against the computer is kept so it can be played again from its start, for sharing and a highlight reel later (the reel design's "Sharing a match against the computer"). `SoloTrail.swift` keeps it beside the save, in Application Support, on the device only. Only the share card reads it, and only when the person presses SHARE AND MAKE THE REEL is anything sent (docs/ONLINE.md "Sharing a match against the computer").

- **While the match is played**, `solo-match-trail.jsonl` holds JSON lines: a head `{"v":1,"engine":"0.7.2","complete":true,"start":"<first snapshot>","h":"<hash>"}`, then one `{"a":<action>,"h":"<hash after it>"}` for each committed action (the person's and the computer's throws, raises, punishments, a resignation; the computer's own raises and handovers come back through the engine). Each action is one append of one whole line, written before the save, so a line costs about 370 bytes and never rewrites the file. A line is read only if it ends in a newline and parses: a kill mid-append loses that line and nothing before it. The hash is the engine's (`hash`, FNV-1a of the snapshot).
- **Resume.** A cold launch that restores the save reads the trail and lines it up by hash: its last action's hash must be the save's (an append the save never caught up with, a kill between the two, is cut back; so is a part line). Then the trail goes on from there and still begins at the match's first snapshot. The engine is the one the save's own files name (`MatchSave.engineNamed`): the trail's, then the finished-match file's (a finished match reopened on its winner card has no trail left), each only when it holds the save's hash, which is FNV-1a of the snapshot text, so no engine is needed to read it; a trail or finished file from another match names nothing. A match named on an engine this build does not carry (one begun on 0.9 by an earlier build) is never played on another: `restore` throws `OldMatch`, changing nothing, and the app drops the save with its trail (`MatchSave.dropOld`), says "This match was made on an older version of the game and can no longer be replayed." and starts afresh (docs/ENGINES.md "Looking back at a match"). A kept match holding a typed name from before generated names still resumes, through the plain `restore`: it is the device's own and only its player sees it. It never leaves the phone with that name: a share renames side A to the name on the card and the engine judges the whole start (`names.check({ replay })`, "This match did not pass the check, so it cannot be shared." when it refuses). A new match and practice are always on the newest. If the trail is missing, unreadable or from another match (a save from a build before the trail), the match resumes on the newest exactly as before; a new trail starts at that checkpoint marked `complete: false`, and the match cannot be shared. `PitchSession.trailComplete` says which. A trail that cannot be written stops being kept for that match; play never stops for it.
- **The finished match.** When the match ends `over`, its trail becomes `solo-match-finished.json`, written atomically in place of the one before: `{"v":1,"engine","complete","winner","throws","name","start","log":[{"a","h"}]}`, with the name side A played under (from the start's options). The trail file goes. It stays through Home (which clears the save and the trail, not this), relaunches and a new match in progress, and is replaced only when the next solo match ends (a resumed pre-trail match then leaves one marked incomplete). If the app is killed after the last save and before this, the next launch finishes it; a launch that restores a finished save with no trail starts none, so it never replaces the finished match.
- **For the share step.** `MatchSave.finishedMatch()` gives it: `start`, `log` (`{ "a": <action> }` each, no hashes) and `replay`, the `{ start, log }` the engine's `replayLoad` takes, with `complete`, `engine`, `winner`, `throwCount` and `name`. The report history is separate and unchanged: a feedback report still starts at the resumed checkpoint.
- **Size.** About 370 bytes an action. The longest seeded match in the tests (club level, the computer against a planned person, 762 actions, 655 throws) leaves a 284 KB finished file, 18 KB after its first 40 decisions; that is beyond the share message's planned 256 KB, while a match of 200 actions would come to about 75 KB at that rate (an estimate, not measured).
- Practice and online play keep no trail: practice has no save, and every online action goes to the room.

## Send feedback

Practice and solo both offer SEND FEEDBACK in the menu (the web's src/ui/report.js).
The card asks what it is about: "A throw looked wrong" (first when the game has a
last throw) or "Something else". A throw report has the two answers and the pieces
to mark: the player can tap several pieces on the overhead pitch or choose their
names when pieces overlap, and the card freezes the last throw's rest positions,
including pieces collected at the end of a turn. Something else is one box of up to
2000 characters. Both send the game as it stood when the card opened, with no name or
contact field. The wire is the web's: `kind: "throw"` with the answers, selected IDs
and poses, or `kind: "feedback"` with a trimmed `text` and no answers; both carry the
replay state. A failed send keeps the draft open. Resuming a solo save starts its
report history at that checkpoint.
When practice history exceeds the report size limit, the report keeps the engine's
pre-throw checkpoint and the action. Replaying the checkpoint restores the surface,
fallen kubbs and previously thrown batons with their full-precision poses.

## Differences from browser solo

Both clients use the same pointer tune, options, computer, rules, raises, punishments and notation. Native controls use a paused match menu, native option pickers, a compact turn rail with batons left and both sides' standing counts, numbered end labels and a smaller schematic minimap. The winner shows hits as a fraction. The sheet exports through Files. The touch name field counts extended grapheme clusters. The browser counts UTF-16 code units.

The app hides the dial in flight, keeps the pitch full height and applies the target pan immediately. The current browser source still draws a promptless dial in playback and eases its aim pan. Quartz wood, ink, chips and text metrics differ slightly from Canvas. Saving and resuming a private match on the phone is deliberate. The sound and haptic cues share the playback clock. See [Pitch feedback](FEEL.md). Browser links to rules and the notation guide, full how-to overlays, keyboard/mouse shortcuts and web release controls are outside the native screen. Simulator timing, physical touch comfort and VoiceOver pitch manipulation still need device evaluation.

## Verification

`SoloTests` plays an uncapped, seeded computer match on both sides through the production controller and a separate engine. It compares the hash at every release and the finished sheet. It also restores an atomic mid-flight save, checks the next release, tests projected legal end selection and punishment refusal/placement, and exercises the actual option, plan and stance calls. `SoloTrailTests` plays a seeded match against the computer through the session with the trail on disk, closes and restores it from the save after 40 decisions, then plays the finished match through the engine's `replayLoad` and `replayStep` to `over` with the same winner and final hash (and with the kept hashes, no action differs); it also covers a cut append, a missing, corrupt or other match's trail, the finished match surviving Home, a relaunch and a new match, and practice and online writing nothing. `StandPadTests` ports the web's stand-pad tests (the plate's span, the advantage line, the map both ways, the words) and checks one `setStance` a frame, the frozen span, the caption's second and the VoiceOver step. `ControlsTests` covers the slider arithmetic (the release range, the side steps, one `setArc` a frame, the step difference), the aim drag's scale and axes, tap-to-target, and automatic raising: its pace (0.4 s first, then half a second a kubb, four seconds at most), every kubb tipped toward and stood on the engine's choice with the hash checked against a second engine after each action, a tap skipping the rest with the same actions in the same order, one VoiceOver line, the computer's raises watched (and stood without a tip under Reduce Motion), and no raise for the other side or under a card. `RaiseFramingTests` holds the raise camera to twice the ground and the kubb's touch target, numbers and panel on the phones and an iPad, upright and on their side, in a crowded raise and beside the sideline; `VerdictLayoutTests` holds the verdict clear of the aim and its time.

The five raw simulator checkpoints in `ThrowingWoodKit/Tests/KubbRendererTests/Fixtures/solo/` come only from a synthetic match. Regenerate them with `node tools/solo-fixtures.mjs <web-checkout>` on engine 0.4.0. A second engine plans the scripted human's throws. Every checkpoint is the original match's untouched snapshot. The fixtures are test resources. Simulator review reads one copied into the app's temporary directory. The installed device app only reads its own local save. `raise-many.json` (two kubbs to raise for the human side) comes from the same synthetic recipe in Swift, seed 1001: `TW_WRITE_RAISE_FIXTURE=1 swift test --package-path ThrowingWoodKit --filter writeRaiseManyFixture`. `raise-refused.json` (a raise whose first end the engine refuses, invented teams, pass and play) comes from the engine's own seeded match: `TW_WRITE_RAISE_FIXTURE=refused swift test --package-path ThrowingWoodKit --filter writeRaiseRefusedFixture`.
