# The offline solo match

The home screen opens practice or a match against the computer. PLAY THE COMPUTER resumes an unfinished solo game or opens its options. START creates a new match. REMATCH returns to the options. The match menu pauses the clock and provides level, Raising, Meter (Clock or Linear), Meter side, score sheet, home and resignation.

A finished match against the computer can be shared and given a highlight reel from its winner card: MAKE A HIGHLIGHT REEL opens the share card, and after a share the same button opens the reel card for the returned code. Nothing is sent before SHARE AND MAKE THE REEL. The card reads the kept finished match (`MatchSave.finishedMatch()`, below) only when it is the match on the screen. docs/ONLINE.md "Sharing a match against the computer".

The phone can be held on its side as well as upright (Jamie, 2026-11-19: "Given we have a widescreen view for web, can we extend that to support iPhone rotated display?"). On its side the match takes the web's wide arrangement: the rail top left, MENU top right, the clock low in the Meter side corner with the plan beside it, the minimap in the other corner. The clock is always shown there, because no phone on its side has the linear meter's room. The match, its save and the engine do not know which way the phone is held. Turning it mid-gesture lets go of the dial, an aim drag, a slider or STAND with nothing sent. docs/RENDER.md "A phone on its side".

## Session contract

- `PitchSession` owns one main-actor Lua state. The app gets the six selectors from `room.constants`. It uses LEVELS for level and MATCH_OPTIONS and OPTION_DEFAULTS for the other five. Club is the web solo default. The host supplies the local Gregorian start date, a random 31-bit seed and a name for each side. Side A plays first and side B is the computer.
- On `new` and `restore`, set control to sides A and CPU true. Adopt the session's `view`. A throw need supplies the thrower, projectile, position in the turn and standing counts. A human handoff pauses until THROW. Lua handles the computer's own raises and handovers. A CPU throw advances through ordinary `frame({dt})` calls including its think and windup.
- While aiming, the pointer dial supplies arm angles. A tap places the target where the finger lands, or on a standing kubb or king whose drawn body is under or within 22 pt of the tap. A drag anywhere on the pitch moves the circle from where it is by the finger's travel, 0.01 m a point along the camera's right and forward laid on the ground, with `setTarget` and no snap; lifting sends the same point once more with the engine's snap. Drag the stance mark directly, or use STAND in the plan (below). After an aim command, read the session's dial so a stationary player sees the updated release ranges before winding back. The session constrains all movement. The minimap tracks the thrower's frame on either side.
- The plan. ARC is a vertical slider over the engine's release range (15 to 165 degrees, `setArc`); a press anywhere on it jumps the knob there and keeps dragging, and the value is sent at most once a display frame. Its header shows the loft (`ARC 46°`), its marks the clock's hours with a long one at the level throw (90). TILT for a baton (5 degrees a step to 20 either way) or SPIN for a kubb (1 a step to 3) is a horizontal slider: the knob follows the finger and settles on the nearest step, sent as the step difference through `stepSide`; the header reads `TILT 10R`, `SPIN 2L` or `TILT 0`. VoiceOver adjusts either one step at a time (`stepArc`, `stepSide`). STAND (`StandPad.swift`, the web's stand-pad.js, which replaced WALK, the Playdate's held A) is the throwing line seen from above: the ground behind it the rules allow, the advantage line when a baton may stand up to it, and a ring where the thrower stands. A press or drag puts the ring under the finger through `setStance`, sent at most once a frame; the plate's ground is fixed at the press so it never shifts under the finger. While it is held and a second after, the plaque says where in words (`1.5 M RIGHT, 1.7 M BACK. STAND ON YOUR LINE OR BEHIND IT.`). VoiceOver adjusts it 0.25 m along the line, with step actions for all four ways. SIDE VIEW (once PLAN VIEW) toggles the engine's side camera, and the same camera shows while either slider is held and 0.5 s after. The plan's row keeps clear of the linear meter (`\.meterRoom`, docs/RENDER.md "The meter"); with the meter on the left the two columns swap so TILT sits beside it. While a slider is held, a one-line plaque under the turn rail says what it does, and TILT draws the baton leaning by the tilt over the circle as the thrower sees it. Nothing else reaches the engine.
- A release has already advanced the match. Save the raw snapshot, play the recording and keep the releasing side's HUD until rest. The controller gets camera and speed from `showAt` and interpolates the recording's poses. SKIP enters the settled view. Rulings, bursts and verdict words come from the engine.
- For `need.kind == raise`, draw the lying kubb and the session's numbered end footprints. The player touches the kubb (`RaiseTouch.swift`, the web's raise-touch.js numbers): a tap on it, or within 24 pt, stands it on the nearer end; a tap near an end's number takes that end; a drag tips it toward the end the finger moves to, drawn with the engine's tip pose, and stands it past half way (short of that nothing is sent). A refused end is never sent: the allowed end lights for a second instead. END 1 and END 2 are VoiceOver actions naming the left and right ends. Match the selected end's `which` to the one-based index in `choices`. An automatic raise omits the index. AUTO raises this kubb by the engine's choice; AUTO ALL, shown while more than one kubb is still to raise, raises the rest of this turn's 0.7 s apart. Raising is the player's choice for the rest of this match, as on the web (auto-raise.js): REST OF GAME on the panel (shown after AUTO ALL, as on the web) and the menu's Raising row (By hand or Automatic, shown once the match has asked this device to raise) do the same for every raise of this device's side; the card says so and offers BY HAND. Nothing is kept on the device. Automatic raising never acts for the other side or under a card, and stops (leaving the kubb to the player) if the engine refuses. For punishment the touch ray meets the ground, the cursor stays within the supplied bounds, and `checkPunish` decides whether PLACE IT is available. Both use `apply` and load the resulting view.
- An over need shows the winner even if a resignation retained a previous handoff in the session. The end card takes its winner, reason and statistics from `need`. The sheet displays `sheet().shown` by default. Show positions, COPY and SAVE use the complete Planet Kubb Notation text returned by the session. SAVE uses the session's filename and the iOS file picker.
- The app writes the opaque UTF-8 snapshot atomically in Application Support at match start and after each committed action. A cold launch restores the session and reestablishes control. During flight the committed throw is already in that file. Cold restoration opens at the next decision. An ordinary background/foreground cycle preserves the in-memory camera and pauses display time. Home keeps an unfinished save for the next PLAY THE COMPUTER. Home after a win clears the finished save.

## The replay trail

A match against the computer is kept so it can be played again from its start, for sharing and a highlight reel later (the reel design's "Sharing a match against the computer"). `SoloTrail.swift` keeps it beside the save, in Application Support, on the device only. Only the share card reads it, and only when the person presses SHARE AND MAKE THE REEL is anything sent (docs/ONLINE.md "Sharing a match against the computer").

- **While the match is played**, `solo-match-trail.jsonl` holds JSON lines: a head `{"v":1,"engine":"0.7.2","complete":true,"start":"<first snapshot>","h":"<hash>"}`, then one `{"a":<action>,"h":"<hash after it>"}` for each committed action (the person's and the computer's throws, raises, punishments, a resignation; the computer's own raises and handovers come back through the engine). Each action is one append of one whole line, written before the save, so a line costs about 370 bytes and never rewrites the file. A line is read only if it ends in a newline and parses: a kill mid-append loses that line and nothing before it. The hash is the engine's (`hash`, FNV-1a of the snapshot).
- **Resume.** A cold launch that restores the save reads the trail and lines it up by hash: its last action's hash must be the save's (an append the save never caught up with, a kill between the two, is cut back; so is a part line). Then the trail goes on from there and still begins at the match's first snapshot. The match resumes on the engine its trail names when this build carries it (docs/ENGINES.md): a match begun on 0.7 by an earlier build plays on on 0.7, with its trail; a new match and practice are always on the newest. If the trail is missing, unreadable, from another match or from an engine this build does not carry (a save from a build before the trail), the match resumes on the newest exactly as before; a new trail starts at that checkpoint marked `complete: false`, and the match cannot be shared. `PitchSession.trailComplete` says which. A trail that cannot be written stops being kept for that match; play never stops for it.
- **The finished match.** When the match ends `over`, its trail becomes `solo-match-finished.json`, written atomically in place of the one before: `{"v":1,"engine","complete","winner","throws","level","name","start","log":[{"a","h"}]}`, with the level the match was set up with and the name typed for side A (from the start's options). The trail file goes. It stays through Home (which clears the save and the trail, not this), relaunches and a new match in progress, and is replaced only when the next solo match ends (a resumed pre-trail match then leaves one marked incomplete). If the app is killed after the last save and before this, the next launch finishes it; a launch that restores a finished save with no trail starts none, so it never replaces the finished match.
- **For the share step.** `MatchSave.finishedMatch()` gives it: `start`, `log` (`{ "a": <action> }` each, no hashes) and `replay`, the `{ start, log }` the engine's `replayLoad` takes, with `complete`, `engine`, `winner`, `throwCount`, `level` and `name`. The report history is separate and unchanged: a feedback report still starts at the resumed checkpoint.
- **Size.** About 370 bytes an action. The longest seeded match in the tests (club level, the computer against a planned person, 762 actions, 655 throws) leaves a 284 KB finished file, 18 KB after its first 40 decisions; that is beyond the share message's planned 256 KB, while a match of 200 actions would come to about 75 KB at that rate (an estimate, not measured).
- Practice and online play keep no trail: practice has no save, and every online action goes to the room.

## Send feedback

Practice and solo both offer SEND FEEDBACK in the menu (the web's src/ui/report.js).
The card asks what it is about: "A throw looked wrong" (first when the game has a
last throw) or "Something else". A throw report has the two answers and the pieces
to mark: the player can tap several pieces on the overhead pitch or choose their
names when pieces overlap, and the card freezes the last throw's rest positions,
including pieces collected at the end of a turn. Something else is one box of up to
2000 characters. Both send the game as it stood when the card opened, with no name or
contact field. The wire is the web's: `kind: "throw"` with the answers, selected IDs
and poses, or `kind: "feedback"` with a trimmed `text` and no answers; both carry the
replay state. A failed send keeps the draft open. Resuming a solo save starts its
report history at that checkpoint.
When practice history exceeds the report size limit, the report keeps the engine's
pre-throw checkpoint and the action. Replaying the checkpoint restores the surface,
fallen kubbs and previously thrown batons with their full-precision poses.

## Differences from browser solo

Both clients use the same pointer tune, options, computer, rules, raises, punishments and notation. Native controls use a paused match menu, native option pickers, a compact turn rail with batons left and both sides' standing counts, numbered end labels and a smaller schematic minimap. The winner shows hits as a fraction. The sheet exports through Files. The touch name field counts extended grapheme clusters. The browser counts UTF-16 code units.

The app hides the dial in flight, keeps the pitch full height and applies the target pan immediately. The current browser source still draws a promptless dial in playback and eases its aim pan. Quartz wood, ink, chips and text metrics differ slightly from Canvas. Saving and resuming a private match on the phone is deliberate. The sound and haptic cues share the playback clock. See [Pitch feedback](FEEL.md). Browser links to rules and the notation guide, full how-to overlays, keyboard/mouse shortcuts and web release controls are outside the native screen. Simulator timing, physical touch comfort and VoiceOver pitch manipulation still need device evaluation.

## Verification

`SoloTests` plays an uncapped, seeded computer match on both sides through the production controller and a separate engine. It compares the hash at every release and the finished sheet. It also restores an atomic mid-flight save, checks the next release, tests projected legal end selection and punishment refusal/placement, and exercises the actual option, plan and stance calls. `SoloTrailTests` plays a seeded match against the computer through the session with the trail on disk, closes and restores it from the save after 40 decisions, then plays the finished match through the engine's `replayLoad` and `replayStep` to `over` with the same winner and final hash (and with the kept hashes, no action differs); it also covers a cut append, a missing, corrupt or other match's trail, the finished match surviving Home, a relaunch and a new match, and practice and online writing nothing. `StandPadTests` ports the web's stand-pad tests (the plate's span, the advantage line, the map both ways, the words) and checks one `setStance` a frame, the frozen span, the caption's second and the VoiceOver step. `ControlsTests` covers the slider arithmetic (the release range, the side steps, one `setArc` a frame, the step difference), the aim drag's scale and axes, tap-to-target, and automatic raising: its gap, every kubb stood by the engine's choice with the hash checked against a second engine, and no raise for the other side or under a card.

The five raw simulator checkpoints in `ThrowingWoodKit/Tests/KubbRendererTests/Fixtures/solo/` come only from a synthetic match. Regenerate them with `node tools/solo-fixtures.mjs <web-checkout>` on engine 0.4.0. A second engine plans the scripted human's throws. Every checkpoint is the original match's untouched snapshot. The fixtures are test resources. Simulator review reads one copied into the app's temporary directory. The installed device app only reads its own local save. `raise-many.json` (two kubbs to raise for the human side) comes from the same synthetic recipe in Swift, seed 1001: `TW_WRITE_RAISE_FIXTURE=1 swift test --package-path ThrowingWoodKit --filter writeRaiseManyFixture`. `raise-refused.json` (a raise whose first end the engine refuses, invented teams, pass and play) comes from the engine's own seeded match: `TW_WRITE_RAISE_FIXTURE=refused swift test --package-path ThrowingWoodKit --filter writeRaiseRefusedFixture`.

See [the review pictures and snapshot command](RENDER.md).
